## Bugs

What happens, where, and the fix if one is known. The evidence behind each line (probes, file:line, transcripts) is in the old ledgers, `git show 0055e6e:ISSUES.md` and `git show 0055e6e:IDEAS.md`, or in the commit the line names.

### Kernel

- **A survivor stalled longer than the knock window turns a clean stop into a crash.** When a member presses Ctrl-C it asks the others to vote it out. Since the knock window (V4-39, [docs/DESIGN-LEAVE.md](docs/DESIGN-LEAVE.md) §10.7) it keeps asking for as long as it is connected to a member that is not itself leaving, up to `leave.ms` (8 s by default: 16 heartbeats, the tune row `leaveWindow`), so a survivor stalled for a few seconds still reads the request and votes, and the stop is clean; with no such member connected it waits four heartbeats and goes, as before. A survivor stalled past the window still wakes to a leaver that is gone and sees a crash; with two members that is permanent. `sim/leave-sweep.test.ts` pins it both ways: with the survivor's process paused (the sweep's stall now) every one of the 100 stall schedules is live at the default window (25 wedged before under that model, 36 under the old slow-survivor model, which stays beside it and wedges 20 now, 1 at a 64-tick window); with the pauses tripled past the window 22 wedge, all safe, and a 64-tick window makes them live again; the 100 cut-and-restart schedules are live on 90 (87 before), the 10 left being pairs cut apart inside the window with nobody else to knock at, a survivor cut from a leaver whose vote it needs, and a restarted leaver beside a read-only survivor, which a longer window does not change. The window has one cost the design did not foresee, the next line. No ballot rule moved with the window. The three splits it left (two suspects evicted at once, the kept ballot, genesis) are closed by the release rule (V4-55, [docs/DESIGN-LEAVE.md](docs/DESIGN-LEAVE.md) §10.10): a vote for a view two or more members apart from the voter's own holds it, read-only and uncounted by any stayer, until that view commits, its proposer disowns the ballot, or that view's own majority supersedes it — never a clock. What that costs is the next line. The parked branch `w1/membership` is history.
- **A held member is read-only until the view it voted for is settled, and three shapes never settle it without an operator.** Since V4-55 a member that voted for a view two or more members apart from its own (a two-suspect eviction, a ballot over an older view, a genesis grant) is `held`: read-only, and not counted toward anyone's majority, until that view commits, its proposer disowns the ballot (it answers the held member's ask with a NACK when it holds neither the ballot nor a view at or above it; the ask goes to the proposer whether or not it is a member), or a ballot that view's own majority consents to supersedes it — when no membership change would open that ballot, the lowest live member opens it, the same members at the next epoch, joint over the holds, only when it can commit and only with every member of its view linked (a lost member's eviction is the ballot instead); it opens it on a hold a linked member named and on its own, when the proposer it granted is out of the view and unlinked (a linked one is asked and disowns). A flap, a crash of the proposer and every one-member change hold nobody, and a disown releases a hold in one round trip. Three shapes stay read-only for good, because from the held member's seat dead and cut look the same (the CP answer, DESIGN-LEAVE §10.10.4): the far side of a cut (`evictTwo`, until the cut heals); the third granter of a two-evictee ballot whose proposer and other granter died for good, with the two evictees it leads (`proposerAndGranterDie`: three live, linked members read-only); and the one survivor of a genesis ballot beside a newcomer or the others reborn memory-only (`genesisProposerAndGranterDie`: nothing forms). The remedy is the operator's, as for the crash-at-two class: forget the dead, or start the survivor fresh. Pinned in `sim/leave-harness.test.ts` as the exact end states. A held member asks its proposer once per heartbeat per member link while held; nothing is sent while nobody holds anything.
- **A LEAVE knock reopens an aged-out leave ballot ahead of an evictable suspect.** A cluster running on a bare majority (three members with one crashed and not yet voted out, five with two), whichever member stops, the lowest included: the lowest stayer's leave ballot leaves the leaver out, so its new set needs a dead member's vote and can never commit. Each time it ages out (`proposalAge`, 10 ticks) the leaver's next knock lands between ticks and re-proposes it before the tick's eviction branch runs, so the dead member is never evicted while the leaver knocks. The leaver waits the whole knock window (about 8.5 s at the default cadence, past `fero.spawn`'s 5 s grace, so the verdict reads `null`) and goes unclean, where before V4-39 it went at the fifth tick; the stayer sits `minority` in the old view either way, so the outcome is no worse, only slower. Measured on the sim at heartbeat 50 with the third member cut 200 ms before the stop: `left` b=0 at 250 / 850 / 3250 ms for a 4 / 16 / 64-tick window; clean in one tick once the eviction ran first. The README says to let the eviction finish first. The fix is proposer logic (a knock must not reopen an aged-out leave ballot while a given-up suspect is evictable), outside the knock window's zero-risk scope: the leave chain's work.
- **After tombstone GC and compaction, a removed row can come back from a short lane.** Three unscoped nodes: one writes a row (its lane stays one record long) and leaves cleanly, and the row moves to another node, which removes it and then writes a hundred rows of its own. Once tombstone GC and compaction both run, the remove is gone from the remover's lane, but the old write is still the last entry of the leaver's lane. So a joiner ends with the row present while the members have it absent, and a durable member comes back from its own disk with it. The release of a stamp has to take every lane's dead entry for that cell with it. Pinned RED TODAY in `sim/scale-probes.test.ts` with no test seam; M1 and M3 see it. The fix moves unscoped trajectories, so it lands by its own decision ([docs/DESIGN-SCALE.md](docs/DESIGN-SCALE.md) §14 Q7).
- **A write from an evicted member beats a newer remove after a heal.** Four unscoped nodes. The owner commits a write whose frames die in the kernel buffer as its links sever, and it is cut off and voted out. Another node removes the row in the next epoch, the three remaining nodes GC the tombstone, and the link heals. The three then hold the old write (stamped in the earlier epoch) and the fourth holds nothing. A remove stamped later lost to a write stamped earlier, and the cluster is split for good. Pinned RED TODAY in `sim/scale-probes.test.ts`: M6 is red at the release itself, M1 and M3 at the heal. Same path to a fix as the short lane: lane accounting before a release.
- **On a scoped cluster, one forwarded write can commit twice across an ownership move.** In the scoped sweep (A3, seed 6) a forwarded field remove commits at the owner; 1.5 ms later the orphan cut takes every one of the owner's links down, before the commit's fan-out or its WACK left it. The issuer retries, the new owner commits it again, and after the heal both commits stand; the attempt-exactly-once monitor fires. The latch is not the gap (it already feeds the dedup window); no other node ever held the first commit. It is the zombie's class, a commit stranded on a cut-off owner, so its fix belongs with the zombie's. A remove committed again can erase a newer write. Pinned in `sim/scoped-sweep.test.ts` KNOWN_UNSAFE. Stage 2 of the scale design (V4-40) did not fix it: A3 seed 6 is safe now only because every scoped trajectory moved and its 1.5 ms window between the commit and the cut does not open; the same class shows at `FERO_SCOPED_SEEDS=12` on A2 seeds 9, 10 and 12 (and on A2 seed 15 on the base tree too), and A2 seed 9 is the pin in `sim/scoped-sweep.test.ts` KNOWN_UNSAFE now, so the class stays in every battery. A3 seed 10 still shows a partition not held where the ring assigns it. The fix is the zombie's.
- **A rolling change of a cluster key or of the secret splits the cluster until every member runs the new value.** The contract refuses a peer whose `heartbeat.ms`, `replicas`, `methods`, `validation` text or ring differs, and the door refuses a peer with another secret, so changing either is a full stop and start of every member and client; there is no rotation window ([docs/DESIGN-DOOR.md](docs/DESIGN-DOOR.md) §4). Loud and correct where the drift used to be silent. The derived client key changes with the secret, so every client restarts too.
- **A discovered target that never admits holds genesis at the lowest-uuid member for good.** Genesis waits there for links ≥ hosts, and hosts counts the discovered set, so a squatter the directory handed out, or a dead entry, blocks the first view until it goes away. A V4-3 property, with or without a door; found by the door family (`sim/door.test.ts`).
- **A dialer that loses the door's refusal notice re-dials a wrong-secret peer for one bounded chain before it gives up.** The DOOR notice is best-effort (on loopback it arrived on 70 of 70 attempts); a bare close while proving is a link loss, so a lost notice costs one redial chain, then the ordinary give-up and heartbeat revival, with no `refused` row on the dialer: the acceptor's `bad-proof` row and line say what happened. In the sim the notice is never read unless the pair's delay is fixed (its close model lands the close before a frame shipped in the same step), so the sim proves the lost-notice path.
- **A client refused `uuid-taken` on the open path redials for good, one journal row per dial.** An open node ships no notice and admits a HELLO on arrival, so a client that holds a member's uuid (a stray `FERO_UUID`) reads a bare close, which is a link loss: it redials at its redial cadence (about every 50 ms at `redial.ms` 30), elects the member as its feeder for a millisecond or two each time and loses it on the close, and the member journals one `doorReject` row per dial beside the link rows, so its 4096-row ring turns over in minutes. Nothing is ever fed; with a secret the client reads the notice and parks, so only the open path loops. Found by the V4-36 review ([docs/DESIGN-DOOR.md](docs/DESIGN-DOOR.md) §3, the dated note). A fix needs a word the open path does not have today, or a park on the dialer's side keyed on a repeated close of an admitted link.
- **A secured crossing dial costs one extra dial.** When two secured members dial each other at once, the dialer whose accepted link the peer closed as the duplicate starts a redial chain before its own dialed link's proof lands; the extra link is closed as a duplicate at once. Harmless, inside the 104,050 pin.
- **The journal's 4096 ring fills in about 34 minutes when two mis-keyed or mis-configured members re-dial each other every 500 ms.** Each dial writes a `doorReject` or `contractReject` row at both ends, the cadence `nameReject` has today. Stated, not fixed.
- **Drain at R=1 when the new holder crashes mid-drain.** The leaver goes at the next tick with `left(false)` and `undrained` counted, and the rows are lost unless it kept a `dir`. Waiting for a re-range while another member is reachable kept every row in a 5-seed probe but ended just inside the 16-tick bound. The owner's call (DESIGN-SCALE's note of 2026-10-10).
- **A scoped leaver whose vote never came back counts nothing.** Still in its own view when its bound ends (`leaveTicks` + 1 with no member linked that could answer, `leaveWindow` + 1 with one), it goes like a crash: nothing froze, so there is no orphan to drain and `undrained` reads 0 even though at R=1 its rows go with it. The README and `api/types.ts` say so (V4-38). A fix needs the leaver to know the ballot's fate, which is the leave chain's work.
- **On an unscoped feeder, a row can still be lost when a spoke fails over between the drain and the capped answer.** V4-49's ceiling caps the drain exchange with what a clear took, so a spliced tap-forward is re-sent; but if the spoke fails over after the drain digest and before the capped answer arrives, the ceiling stays on the old link and the new feeder answers the spoke's own tails, which sit above the hole. Found by the last review of V4-49; older than it (the base loses more). A fix carries the ceiling into the failover's resync, or has a spoke whose link held a ceiling resync on failover.
- **A methods test failed once in its setup and hung the battery.** In the wave-5f merged battery, api/methods.test.ts 'a sub that fails AFTER its first frame' failed in 3.5 ms, before any of its steps; the detail was lost because the file then never exited: every test there built its servers with `pair()` before its own try, so the failed setup left a server listening, and the battery sat for about seven hours. `pair()` now destroys what it started before it rethrows, so such a failure is loud and fast. The cause of the one failure is not proven (most likely a free port taken between picking and binding; it passed in every lane battery and in five lone runs). A battery-wide guard (a per-file time limit, or `--test-force-exit`) would bound any such hang; not adopted, since it would also hide leaks the suites should catch.
- **A spoke that fails over onto a feeder that is repairing a partition, or holding it as an orphan, loses that partition's rows from its screen at the resync's end, until the fold forwards them back.** The failover's resync answer carries what the new feeder feeds at that moment (V4-53); the rows it lacks (a lost frame's delta it is about to fold) or withholds (an orphan's frozen copy, a latch re-entering from one) are not in it, and the spoke had them from its first feeder, so the ghost sweep at the end removes them; the fold's forward brings them back. Sighting 3's schedule (`sim/scale-probes.test.ts`) with the cut 0-30 ms after the latch, 56 runs: the sweep took 7 to 20 of forty cells on 22, the spoke mirrored its feeder at the horizon on all 56; M11 (resync-behind) fires when the fold lands while the reply is in flight (seed 2 cut 1 ms later, seed 6 cut 10 ms later; pinned as KNOWN). Older than V4-53: the base tree fires M11 on 8 of the 56 and never brings the rows back on 23. A fix carries an open latch's staged records in the answer, or keeps the spoke's rows across the end until the fold lands.
- **A spoke keeps showing the rows of a partition that moved off its feeder.** When a join moves a partition off the spoke's feeder, the feeder's copy freezes into an orphan and is shed, but nothing tells the spoke: its rows stay on the screen, frozen (the feeder gate-drops every new record of that partition, so none reaches the spoke), until a failover's resync wipes them. Found by M12's other direction (`spokeStale`, sim/monitors.ts): in the scoped sweep's A12 family the spoke kept 6, 5, 1, 2 and 1 such rows at the horizon on seeds 3, 5, 6, 8 and 11 (seed 2 kept 1 until V4-55 moved its join and leave through the new ballot shape; seed 1 none); pinned as a known count on seed 3, which runs in every battery (`sim/scoped-sweep.test.ts` ALWAYS; 0 means it closed). "Spokes subscribe to what they watch" is the idea that changes it; a shed that tells its spokes is the small fix.
- **A spoke can show a removed row for a few events when its feeder acquires that partition.** The forward of a latch fold (V4-53) ships the partition's history in lane order, one frame per contiguous run, so an older value of a row and its later remove on one lane arrive in separate events while the spoke's fence already sits above both; until the remove lands the spoke shows the row — one event per frame between them (A12 seed 4: p27 at two older stamps in two events). The resync re-download flicker's shape. Before V4-53 the spoke showed nothing of that partition, for ever. M1's per-event form reads no coverage off a spoke of a scoped feeder; M12 judges the spoke at the quiet point.
- **A teacher record above the cut that also staged live goes into the feeder's lane twice.** The ACQDONE fold splices it, and the live replay's `inbound` appends it again: the fold raises `d.applied` only to the cut and the fast path checks the fence, not the lane (lane bb at aa reads [3, 5, 7, 7] in the wave-6a review's probe; the base tree has the same lane). Benign today — a teach re-ships both and the learner's fold skips the second; the forward (V4-53) ships it twice and the spoke drops the copy — but a lane-invariant break for stage 3's compaction, which must not land before it is fixed.
- **After every member stops cleanly one at a time, only the last to stop can bring the cluster back.** Each earlier leaver comes back outside the last view it saw, and gets back in only by a JOIN through a live member of that view (`kernel/membership.ts`, the tick's rejoin and JOIN), so with the first two back and the last still down the pair stays read-only for good and prints nothing. Starting all three works (writable in 1.3 to 1.9 s), and so do any two after `kill -9` of all three (0.9 s). Found 2026-10-11 by the platform spikes (`~/.claude/plans/fero-platform/spikes/local/r2/`).
- **A client stays on a feeder whose writes are shut.** It moves to another server only when its link dies (`kernel/node.ts`, the client primary failover in `linkDown`), though it already knows its feeder's gate (`feederOpen`). Fed by a member the others had refused, a client timed out 80 of 80 calls over 20 s while two members took writes, and all 80 writes landed later. Found 2026-10-11 by the platform spikes (`spikes/local/r2/`).
- **A fresh member's `data:` seed brings back a seed row the cluster deleted, and the copies then disagree.** Three members share a seed; one row is deleted and its tombstone is collected (`tombstoneGC`, within a heartbeat); a fourth member with an empty fold applies the same seed at boot and joins. After it joins, the three old members show the deleted row again and the new member does not, at one view of four, writable, in 2 runs of 2 on v4 63e0679. The seed's own rule ("fresh keys ride in", `api/index.ts` at the seed) cannot tell a key the cluster never had from one it deleted. Every scale-out of a seeded resource hits it. Found 2026-10-12 by the platform page's review (`~/.claude/plans/fero-platform/spikes/review/probe-seed.ts`).
- **A paused link's queue is bounded by count, not bytes.** It holds up to 256 frames (the tune row `queued`) before it clears. Since V4-51 cuts records into frames of up to 256 MiB, a paused link can hold gigabytes before that. Older than V4-51; big values make it reachable. A fix bounds the queue by bytes too. Found by V4-51's build (2026-10-12).
- **An issuer or origin name is not bounded in size.** Every frame that carries a record carries its origin, and a committed attempt also its issuer. A client that names itself with a string near one frame in size makes its records unsendable once they are stamped (a loud throw). Hostile clients only; older than V4-51. A fix refuses such a name at HELLO and at the relay. Found by V4-51's build (2026-10-12).

### The api

- **An array-valued row's screen can still depend on arrival order.** V4-18 folds object, number, null and string rows the same everywhere. data, though, writes into arrays like objects: an index write replaces the element, a field inside an element lands, and a non-index key becomes a hidden property; and data refuses to remove an array's own key, so the kernel makes no repair inside an array. Write the spliced array. Removing an array's own element is refused before it commits, for the shape the issuing node shows, or, when a scoped member cannot see the row, for the shape its owner shows: the lens throws for `delete data.g[0]`, and `methods.remove({ key: ['g', 0] })`, a client's relayed remove or a scoped non-holder's forwarded remove rejects `sparse-hole` (V4-28; the owner re-check of V4-33). One gap still commits a tombstone that shows nothing: a remove sent in the same batch as a still-pending write that turns the row into an array is judged on the old shape.
- **A spoke's write can settle before its value shows when its feeder dies between the WACK and the tap and the next feeder lags the record.** A spoke is a client fed by one scoped server. Since V4-43 its feeder names the owner's lane in the receipt (`l`), and at the failover's resync end the spoke settles, through `acked`, every write its new feeder's download did not bring. A next feeder that holds the partition but has not yet received the record looks the same as one that does not hold it, so the write resolves first and the value shows as long afterwards as that feeder lags the record (nothing bounds it; 43-48 ms in the pinned schedule). A later write of ours to the same cell can also resolve before its value shows (narrow: the newer WACK must overtake the older record and the two taps land in separate steps). Keeping V4-7's order needs a new piece of protocol: the spoke must learn whether its new feeder holds the partition. Pinned by `sim/feeder-death.test.ts` 'known cost'.
- **On the open path, a dup-link close of my own dial starts a generic redial chain toward a member I am still linked to.** The peer can close my duplicate dial before its HELLO reaches me, so the close looks like a link loss and arms the ordinary ten-try chain toward a live peer; each try is another duplicate dial. It is bounded and no longer affects eviction: if the member then crashes, its linkDown replaces that chain with the member chain (V4-34). Pinned by kernel/node.test.ts 'a member that dies while a GENERIC chain toward it is running'.
- **A view a handler makes some other way than ctx.data is not tracked.** Release by the collector (V4-54) covers views made through ctx.data and views made off them. A handler that closes over the resource and calls f.data.filter(…) still leaves that view behind when it returns a plain value. When it returns the view, the old eager release runs, cousin included: a view cached that way and returned on the first call goes stale. A raw handle taken from the internals door (`unwrap`) is never marked as held by outside code, so a deduped view reached that way can be released under it. Evidence: api/views.test.ts covers ctx.data only; disposeMinted in api/substrate.ts is the old path.
- **Under a view module code ever reached, a handler's view goes only if data never reuses its operator.** Once module code touches f.data.top(10), even once, a handler's ctx.data.top(10).sum(fn), .distinct(fn), .some(fn) or .length(fn) stays for good. Data does not reuse those either, but fero picks the private operators by name (filter, map, reduce, group, tap, to, union, intersect, except: PRIVATE_OPS in api/substrate.ts), so any other operator is treated as one module code might reach. It is never released early, only never. Release also waits for the collector: a dropped view is worked out on every commit until a GC finds it.
- **A view a handler passes as a reactive argument can stay for good.** In `ctx.data.gt('n', ctx.data.avg('n'))` data hangs an effect on the avg, so the collector refuses it while the gt lives; when the gt goes later nothing tries the avg again, and it stays on the source (a per-key cache of 23 such chains, cleared, left 46 nodes where v4 left 69). Late, never early. A fix remembers the argument views a call passed in and tries them when the view they feed is released. Found by the third I-29 review (2026-10-11).
- **After data cuts a write loop, this node shows one write less than its kernel holds.** A tap that writes on every change runs until data's cap (1000 commits); data drops the queued projections, so the proxy reads the second-to-last value while the kernel and every other node hold the last, until the row changes again. Older than V4-47; since then fero says so in one stderr line instead of throwing at the assignment. A fix refuses a runaway chain of tap-issued writes in Bind.issue, below data's cap, before the kernel stamps them.
- **The refusal lines can skip a line on a journal ring overflow.** They are fed from the composer's post-step journal cursor, so a burst past `journal.cap` inside one step loses the line; the row is the contract.
- **A row read by reference can be changed in place, and then the copies disagree.** `f.snapshot`, `await data` on an unscoped node, and the rows a view hands out are the stored values themselves, not copies. A program that changes `f.snapshot.r.n` changes what this node holds and serves from then on, because its kernel and its log hold the same object; every other copy keeps the old value (in V4-52's design probe the writer read 1, the server and a late client 99). Since V4-52 a write keeps fero's own copy, so this is the one way left for a stored value to change. A fix freezes or copies rows where they are handed out; it costs every read, so measure that first. Found by V4-52's design (2026-10-12).
- **A keyed read during a handover can be refused while a member's whole share is bigger than one frame.** Such a read (this node owns the row but is still taking it over) gathers every member's whole share and picks its row; a member whose share passes one frame refuses its answer `too-large`, so the read is refused until the handover ends, and its hint says to retry in a moment. A fix asks members for that key's partition only, in a fold of its own beside the live union. Found by the V4-51/52 fix check (2026-10-12).

- **V4-44** A sim monitor pass reads only the nodes a step changed: LOCAL monitors walk the stepped nodes in node order and keep owing the ones they did not reach; LEDGER monitors read the events that touched the ledger; a pass after run(), at() or a new Sim reads every node; `Sim.fullWalk` keeps the old walk and a 47-test suite proves both walks report the same violations; a pass at n=100 costs 0.13 ms where it cost 15 ms; no trajectory moved.
- **V4-45** The directory's leftovers: IPv4-mapped loopback reads as loopback everywhere; a failed lookup reopens with a doubling jittered delay and its error clears only when the lookup itself answers; the host closes a client link silent for 20 heartbeats (a hung registrant's entry goes with it; a live one is never closed); one directory client per process, refcounted, with an owner-only unregister at a stop; the I-19 race probe did not reproduce.
- **V4-46** The debugger judges each row among its holders, each client by its feeder, a member that left by its tenure, and each resource on its own; the capture carries `held` and `feeder`; the DIAG wire (DIAG_P 2) sends journal deltas from a cursor, caches log entries exactly through a new seqs read, and reads a big state in pages with a torn-snapshot marker; nothing on the kernel's hot path.
- **V4-47** The user's own code never stops fero. A view's function, a tap or a subscriber that throws because fero changed a source it watches: data leaves the row out of a filter or map and stops any other view (its SCHEDULE_VERSION 5), fero prints one stderr line per bug, with where the code threw, and carries on, and a live method subscription over a stopped view fails with the reason. A `validation:` rule that throws on a client's record refuses it `validation-threw`; a method result the wire cannot carry rejects that call.
- **V4-48** A value fero cannot send is refused before it is stamped: a `bigint`, a function, a symbol, a getter that throws, a live view, deeper than 60 levels from the row (the deepest built-in read wraps four more), bigger than 255 MiB. An assignment throws and says where (`a bigint at toys[1]`), `methods.*` rejects `unencodable` with the reason, a `data:` seed fails fero() naming the row. A method argument fero cannot send fails the call instead of ending the client. Anything of the user's that fails in a seed fails fero() named, with the teardown; a rule that answers `false` or nothing refuses the write, with that as the reason. The encoder reads arrays by index.
- **V4-49** A clear on a synced client link leaves a ceiling on the link: each lane lowered to just under the lowest record the clear took; the spoke's reply to the drain digest is answered from the lower of its tails and the ceiling, so a spliced tap-forward is re-sent; member links and the sim unchanged.
- **V4-50** The debugger with a node missing from a live capture tells one story (a client whose feeder is missing is not judged, and the header says so; a holder still copying a row in keeps its lag signal from the open acquire), and the rewind folds a hand-off's records at the copy that brings them, trailing the kernel but never leading it.
- **V4-51** Records are cut into frames by size where they are grouped: the link's outbox, the digest answer, the commit's WAL rows, the snapshot, WRITE and ACQ frames. A flush that clears a member link's queue ships nothing more of it. A read too big for one frame (a whole read, a live read's first picture or one of its changes, a gathered share) is refused typed — ScopedError reason `too-large`, with a hint — never cut or dropped. The relay refuses before the stamp a client record it could not send alone. A split frame arrives in linear time. The shell writes WAL rows at their exact size and streams the snapshot file. The tune row `frame` is the test seam, with no config key.
- **V4-52** A write keeps fero's own copy of its value (`copyOf`, made by the walk that checks it, as the decoder would make it), so a later change to the program's object reaches no copy. A value that would arrive changed — a `Date`, a `Map`, a `Set`, other typed arrays, a Proxy, a built-in that keeps its data out of sight, a key with half of a surrogate pair — is refused at the write like a `bigint`, with what to send instead; a class the program wrote is taken as its own fields, unless it extends such a built-in. A server's own calls and reads answer what a client's call decodes, refused `too-large` only by the reply's real size. A method handle sends its argument as it was when it was made. An insert under a row is refused `invalid-key` before the stamp, and one already stamped folds as that field. The decoder keeps a leading U+FEFF.
- **V4-53** The latch forwards to the spokes: a synced spoke holds the whole of every partition its feeder FEEDS (held, or acquiring from a held base or from nothing; only an orphan's frozen copy, and a latch re-entering from one, is withheld), the client's digest answer walks the lanes with that rule, the ACQDONE fold forwards every record it takes in to each synced client link exactly as the live tap does, and a re-entry from an orphan forwards the whole partition and lowers a stopped answer's cover; M12 spoke-mirror is the oracle for spokes on a scoped cluster (A12 and A13 the spoked families); stage 2 did not open the hole, the latch always had it.
- **V4-54** A handler's views are released when the collector finds the handler let go of them: ctx.data is a scope over the surface (one object per server, not f.data itself) that wraps every view it makes and calls data's own operator (no "handler is running" flag); a FinalizationRegistry disposes a view once its last wrapper is collected (not if it has a child, a subscription, or module code can reach it); a view data never reuses is private even under one module code holds; a view returned through ctx.data waits for the collector too (the proxy-return cousin is fixed); the collector walks through a view data stopped (V4-47) to the views under it.
- **V4-55** The release rule, built (DESIGN-LEAVE §10.10): a vote for a view two or more members apart from the voter's own holds it — read-only (`held`), uncounted by any stayer, leading and granting only a ballot joint over that view; the release is the proposer's disown (NACK) where the proposer can be asked, on its link member or not, and supersession by the held view's own majority where it cannot — opened by the lowest live member when no membership change would, on a linked member's hold or its own, with every member of its view linked — never a clock; the holds ride the HELLO (`v`, signed under `fero-door/2`) and the PULL (`vs`); a PROPOSE carries its base epoch (`o`) and a ballot over a view below the voter's is refused; no ballot outlives an install; the four splits of the base close and the cost is the CP answer in three shapes, pinned.
