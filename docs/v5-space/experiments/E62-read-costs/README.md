# E-62: what the read surface still owed, timed

**The question, in our words.** V5-13, V5-15 and V5-16 took the read surface's shape but left six of its costs unpriced ([LAYERS.md](../../LAYERS.md) section 11, "Owed for the read surface" items 5, 6, 8 and 10 and "Owed for V5-15" items 1 and 5; [API.md](../../API.md) 11.2). What does each cost, on the same prototypes the pages already cite, does it meet its bar, and which line of API.md section 4 or of LAYERS.md would it change? This folder only measures and says; it edits no page.

1. `c.version`, `c.cursor` and a diff entry's `version`, each read in E-55 claim 10's harness (bar: 50 ns a commit).
2. Counting back for `-N` on a real log in E-19's slot layout, with a cold partition beside a hot one.
3. The `ended` guard in a real read trap, on E-55 claim 1's harness.
4. The per-row version kept inside the row's own entry in `data`'s store, against E-56's separate `Map`.
5. The `equals` guard at the leader: a deep compare, the value's text or a hash of it, and its bytes on the wire, beside a version guard.
6. A field's `-N`: the row's log read back past a busy sibling field.

Revised after a review ([The review](#the-review-what-held-and-what-changed)): items 1, 2, 3 and 6 were changed and run again; items 4 and 5 were not run again, and only their words changed. Three answers moved: `c.version` misses the bar once a slot has moved; a count back is tenths of a ms when its records are not in the caches, not tens of µs; and the `ended` guard costs a few ns, not nothing.

**The short answer.**

1. **`c.version` and a diff entry's `version` are about at the 50 ns bar at epoch 0 and miss it once a slot has moved (by 1.2 to 1.5 times alone, 2.3 to 3.1 times end to end), unless the epoch's digits are made once per slot. A one-partition `c.cursor` misses in both text forms tried. A whole-resource `c.cursor` misses by hundreds of times if built every commit** *(measured)*. `c.version` built alone: 24.9 to 50 ns at epoch 0, 60.3 to 77.1 at epoch 3, 34.6 to 57.4 at epoch 3 with the epoch's digits made once; end to end +39 to +76.5, +114 to +155 and +42.5 to +81 ns a commit, where two copies of one way differ by up to 30.7 ns. E-56's BigInt form: 166 to 175 ns. A one-partition cursor, kept on the commit and made one flat string: 68.2 to 106 ns in E-56's layout, 61.9 to 72.3 as the link's prefix plus the version text (129 to 166 at epoch 3). A whole-resource cursor: 18,624 to 26,768 ns a read as text.
2. **Counting back `-5` with a slot index costs about 0.35 to 0.43 ms at 10^6 partitions when its records are not in the CPU's caches (p90 0.65 to 0.69 ms), and 42 to 43 µs when they are. That is 1.4 to 6 times under CONNECT's 0.6 to 2 ms, with each key compared as bytes. A cold partition beside a hot one reads every slot record since its 5th last write, up to 27 to 32 ms here. A gap back to the partition's previous record, carried in each record on top of the slot index, makes any count back 5 reads, 17 to 23 µs cold** *(measured)*. With v4's decoder in place of the byte compare, the count would be inside CONNECT's range *(reasoned)*. With no slot index: 173 to 181 ms at 10^6. The gap costs 1.0 to 1.5 B a record; a gap in bytes, which would need no slot index, 2.9 to 3.6 B *(counted)*.
3. **The `ended` guard is cheap, not free: +2.6 to +15 ns a read in the forms a build with many links would use** *(measured)*: one trap closure per handle +5.0 to +15, one handler shared by every handle +2.6 to +15, with one key at 10^3 to 10^6 rows. One closure over one handle, a form only one link can have, costs +0.3 to +0.9 at 10^3 rows. E-56's second `Proxy` costs +16 to +30 with one key. Against claim 1's 150 ns a step, the guard is small.
4. **A version kept in the row's own entry costs a write nothing measurable, where the separate `Map` costs +8 to +397 ns, and +33 to +476 once versions are above 2^31 (any slot that has moved). It takes 8 to 16 B a row against 29 to 53** *(measured)*. Read alone by key it is 4 to 100 ns slower than the `Map`, about what reading the row costs.
5. **An `equals` guard is cheap for small values: a deep compare costs 1 ns for a number, 0.23 µs for a 108 B row, 0.35 to 0.40 µs for 1 KB, 24 µs for 11 KB, and decoding the old value from the request costs more than comparing it** *(measured)*. The value rides the request (4 B to 10.7 KB). A hash of the value saves bytes but costs the leader more than the compare, and it refused 1,000 of 1,000 equal values whose keys were in another order *(checked)*: not worth it. A version guard costs about what reading the field costs. A deep compare in a link (ANYPATH T11) was not run; from these compares it would be about 0.23 µs a link a whole-row record for a row-sized value *(reasoned)*.
6. **A field's `-5`, read back by the serving copy, costs the row's records back to the field's 5th change: 9 to 41 µs when the field changes once in 10 writes of its row, 0.15 to 0.39 ms once in 1,000. Read forward over the same window with the same key filter, it costs about the same. Folded forward through data it costs 7 to 41 times more, because every sibling record is decoded and written, not because of the direction** *(measured)*.

## Words

- **Partition**: the rows one leader keeps in one order. At `depth: 1` a row is its own partition; at `depth: 2` a room is. **Slot**: one of 4,096 hash buckets a partition falls in; each slot keeps its own count of records, its **sequence**, and an era number, its **epoch**. The epoch is 0 until the slot first moves, is restored or recovers uncleanly (CONNECT 3.1); after that it stays above 0.
- **Commit**: one leader's batch of writes, as a link sees it. **Version**: a row's (epoch, sequence) at its last write, as short text that keeps log order, such as `62345D0` at epoch 0 and `93002345D0` at epoch 3 (E-56's form). **Cursor**: a reader's place, one (epoch, sequence) per slot it reads, plus the resource's id.
- **Link**: one live `connect` in this process. **Tap**: E-55's name for a callback link. **Diff entry**: fero's own object for one changed row in `c.diff`.
- **Rope**: a string made by `+` that the engine keeps in pieces until someone reads its characters; **flat**: made one piece. Whoever sends, saves or compares a string pays to make it flat.
- **Read trap**: the `get` function of the `Proxy` that a partition's live view is. **The `ended` guard**: the check that makes a read through an ended link's view throw `ended` (V5-13 pick 5).
- **Count back, `-N`**: find the N-th last commit that reached a lens. **Slot index**: each slot's records listed in log order, so a slot's sequence finds its record. **Prev gap**: a number in each record that says how far back, in its slot's sequence, its partition's previous record is. **Byte gap**: the same distance in bytes.
- **Cold / warm read**: the first read right after the CPU's caches are cleared / the reads right after it, with the records still in the caches.
- **Guard**: a condition the leader checks before it applies a write. **`equals` guard**: "apply only if the field still equals this value" (V5-15 pick 5). **Version guard**: `if: { version }`, "only if the row's version is still this".
- **Hot / cold partition**: one that takes many of its slot's records / one written long ago and not since.

**Marks.** *(measured)*: timed, three runs, each process alone on the box under the timing lock; the range over the runs. *(checked)*: a check in the bench passed, and its line is in `results/`. *(counted)*: an exact count from the bench, not a time. *(reasoned)*: an argument, nothing ran it. *(a guess)*: nobody measured it.

## How each was measured

Every timed process ran alone on the box under the timing lock (one hold for a set of processes, run one after another). Inside a process the ways are made up front and run round by round, the order turned by one each round, with a full collection before each; a difference is taken inside a round, and a process gives the median of its rounds (E-55 claim 10's interleaved form). A cell below is the range of those medians over three runs. Where a bench has a "noise" line, it is two copies of the same way.

**Alone on the box, checked.** Every line written since the review carries `box`: the CPU time the whole box spent busy outside the process while it ran (from `/proc/stat`, less the process's own CPU time over all its threads). A line where other processes used a tenth of a CPU or more, on average, is set aside by `summarize.mjs`, and `./run.sh again <bench>` adds a run in its place. One fields run was set aside: other processes used 7 to 77 CPU seconds while its three processes ran, and `./run.sh again fields` ran in its place. Every line kept saw at most 4.1 CPU seconds from other processes, under 8 % of one CPU on average ([results/summary.md](results/summary.md), last section). Items 4 and 5 ran before this check existed.

- **Item 1.** E-55's prototype has no log, so the writer makes each write's frame header (its slot, and the slot's epoch and sequence, which rise by 1 to 3 since other partitions share the slot) and E-55's delivery is replaced, in this process only, by one that hands the header to the commit object. One record per commit. `c.version` is E-56's length-prefixed base32 text, built from the two numbers when first read, without BigInt, and checked against E-56's BigInt form on 10^6 seeded pairs. Every version field is timed at epoch 0, the shortest text (`62345D0` here), and at epoch 3 (`93002345D0`): a slot's epoch rises at its first move, restore or unclean recovery and never falls back. At epoch 3 it is also timed with the epoch's digits and the length character made once per slot and epoch, so a call writes only the sequence's 8 digits (`vtextP`, checked against the plain text on 10^6 seeded pairs). A one-partition `c.cursor` comes in two text forms, each kept on the commit when first read and made flat: E-56's layout (the 16-byte id, then varints; 25 B here) as base64url; and the link's own prefix (id and slot, made once per link) followed by the version text, another format. A diff entry is fero's own object per change (E-56 probe 3 built one in 21 to 29 ns), with its version built when read. *part*: one room of 10,000 rows at depth 2, 100,000 field writes a round over every row, 21 rounds, one tap on the room, so the link sits inside one partition, where `c.version` exists. *whole*: claim 10's own shape, the tap on a resource of 10,000 rows at depth 1; there a cursor is one sequence per slot, 4,096 slots, kept four ways (a live array and its text built per read; a 32 KB copy per read; a persistent 16-ary trie path-copied per record, whose root is the cursor). Unlike E-55's `tap2`, every way runs on one resource and one tap and only switches what the tap reads: with a resource per way, two copies of the same way differed by more than the bar in trial runs (not kept here, so not evidence). *alone*: each field built on 100,000 captured commit objects in a tight loop, against a loop that only clears what the commit keeps (E-56 probe 3's "alone" column).
- **Item 2.** A log of 4 or 8 million records in E-19's batches of 256: per record an issuer, a call id, the slot and the slot's sequence (varints), the gap back to the partition's previous record in the slot (a varint), a 3-byte payload length, then the payload: the key path first, each key as a length and its bytes, then the value as JSON. 4,096 slots. A slot index lists each slot's records in log order, as PATHS PA-12 has the log index by slot. A commit is a batch, so `-5` is the 5th last batch that holds a record of the partition. Four ways to find it: *slot* (walk the slot index newest first, compare each record's partition key as bytes), *slot+dec* (the same, each key decoded to a string first), *scan* (no slot index: walk every batch newest first and read every header), and *prev* (start from the partition's newest version and hop back by the gap; each hop turns a sequence into an offset through the slot index, CONNECT 3.4's "cheap bound"). The log also counts, without writing it, what a byte gap would take instead. *even*: 10^4, 10^5 or 10^6 partitions, every record to a random one; 50 targets picked at random among the partitions that hold at least 5 commits (so `-5` exists), and 20 others, never timed, that warm the code. *hot*: 10^5 partitions, one hot partition takes 1 % or 10 % of all records, and three cold partitions in its slot were written 5 times at an age of 10^5, 10^6 and 3.9 × 10^6 records, and never since. Each way's first read of a target comes right after the caches are cleared: the bench reads one byte in every 64 of a 1 GB buffer, twice the 504 MiB last-level cache this box reports. That first read is the cold time, what a reader that opens once pays. The median of the 5 reads after it is the warm time. Every way must land on the same batch (checked for every target).
- **Item 3.** Claim 1's read, `rows[k].n`, through a handle on the whole resource (E-55's `View` trap), one key and keys spread, at 10^3, 10^5 and 10^6 rows; 200,000 reads a round, 9 rounds. The guard is `if (handle.dead) throw ended` in the trap, three ways: one trap closure over one handle; one trap closure per handle, 1,000 handles made by one function, one of them read; and one handler shared by every handle, with the handle on the Proxy's target. Before any way is made, a spare handle of each kind is ended, so `dead` is a field that changes, as in any process where some link has ended. Beside them E-56's guard (a second Proxy over the view) and a row link's value getter with the same check. After the run the timed guards are ended and must throw `ended`, and another handle's view must still read.
- **Item 4.** [patch/data-ver.patch](patch/data-ver.patch) adds to data's `Store` a `Float64Array` beside `slots`, indexed by the same slot, and lets `SourceNode.write(key, path, value, at, ver)` store `ver` right after the one key probe the write already makes, also when the write changes nothing (a row's version is its last write's). One store of 10^3, 10^5 or 10^6 rows (E-05's small row), each write to a seeded random row: a whole row made before the clock (as E-56's tree bench) or a field set to a new number. The ways share the store: a plain write, a plain write then `Map.set` (E-56's `ver`), the version in the entry, both again with versions above 2^31, and data as it is on its own store of the same rows. 100,000 writes a round, 21 rounds. Then the read of one row's version, a Map get against the entry's (the key probe, then the column). Checked: after a write to every row through both, the column equals the Map.
- **Item 5.** A store of 1,000 rows, each with a field `v` holding one kind of value, decoded from JSON as a fold makes it; each guarded write names a row in turn and carries the old value as a decoded copy (equal, but other objects). The leader reads the field and compares: a deep compare (objects by key set, arrays in order); the JSON text of the value now against the request's text; xxHash32 under two seeds (64 bits, E-05's), Node's native `zlib.crc32`, or SHA-256 over that text, against a hash in the request. The version way reads the row's version from a Map, in place of the field, against the request's version. Kinds: a number, a 16-character string, E-05's small row (108 B of JSON), the same padded to about 1 KB, a list of 100 small rows (about 11 KB). Each with the guard passing (the whole compare runs) and refused at the last field. 9 rounds. Checked: every way agrees with the deep compare; and on equal values with their keys in another order, which way passes.
- **Item 6.** The log of item 2, 2 × 10^6 records over 10^5 partitions; one row `w` (depth 1, so its own partition) takes 1 % of them: writes to its field `f`, to its sibling `g` (10, 100 or 1,000 of them per write to `f`), and whole-row writes (0 % or 2 % of the row's writes; half of them change `f`). V5-15 pick 2: `-5` on a field counts the commits whose records reached it, at or under it, or above it and changing its value. *back*: the serving copy walks the row's slot back through the slot index; a record of `w` has its second key read (`g`: skipped unread; `f`: its value decoded; none: the whole row decoded for `f`), and a record changed `f` when its value differs from the next older one, so it reads one record past the window, to the older value. *fwd*: the same window read forward with the same key filter, its start and the value before it given. *fold*: LAYERS 10 item 39's reading, the same window of the row's records folded forward through data's store with data's rule on `f`, its start given; every record of `w` in the window, the sibling's too, is decoded and written. Times are warm: the median of 7 after a warm-up. Checked: `fwd` and the fold each see exactly 5 changes.

- **`c.version` is about at the bar at epoch 0, and misses it once a slot has moved.** At epoch 0 the text is short (5 to 7 characters here): 24.9 to 50 ns alone, +39 to +76.5 end to end, where two copies of the same way differ by up to 30.7 ns. ## The review: what held and what changed

A review read every bench against the pages, ran four of this folder's cells and four probes again under the timing lock (three runs each), and raised 13 findings. Each was checked against the benches and the pages before anything changed: all held, the last in part.

1. **`c.version` was timed at epoch 0 only, the shortest text.** Held: the writer's header had `epoch: 0`, and so did every commit timed alone. *Changed:* item 1 times epoch 3 too, end to end and alone, and a faster text with the epoch's digits made once per slot. *Now:* about at the bar at epoch 0, 1.2 to 1.5 times it alone and 2.3 to 3.1 times it end to end at epoch 3, near it again with the faster text.
2. **The prefix cursor's 25 to 29 ns was an artifact.** Held: it kept nothing on the commit and never made its rope flat (`.length` does not), so it cost less than `c.version` alone, which it contains. The same was true of the E-56 text with its fixed part made once. *Changed:* both are kept on the commit and made flat, and the prefix form is timed end to end. *Now:* both cursor forms miss.
3. **"The `ended` guard is free" was a best case.** Held: one closure over one handle. *Changed:* a closure per handle (1,000 handles) is timed beside the shared handler, and the `dead` field is made mutable first. *Now:* +2.6 to +15 ns a read, "cheap". The review's own probe showed the field's mutability was not why the best case was near 0; it is the one closure.
4. **Count-back times were warm, and the targets busier than typical.** Held. *Changed:* every way's first read after the caches are cleared is reported, beside the warm median; targets are picked among all partitions with 5 commits, not those with 10 records; the code is warmed on other partitions. *Now:* 0.35 to 0.43 ms cold at 10^6 partitions over 1,154 records, not "tens of µs".
5. **The prev gap is not an alternative to the slot index.** Held: `byPrev` turns a sequence into an offset through the slot index. *Changed:* the words say "on top of the slot index", and the log counts what a byte gap, which needs no slot index, would take: 2.9 to 3.6 B a record.
6. **30 to 50 ns a key rests on this folder's payload**, the partition key first as bytes. Held. *Changed:* "under CONNECT's estimate" is now tied to that DEV-09 choice. Cold, the key compare is no longer the main cost, so the gap to CONNECT shrank anyway.
7. **"A version guard costs nothing measurable" was wrong.** Held: the version way reads a `Map` in place of the field, so its column is one read less another. *Changed:* "about what reading the field costs".
8. **"Folding forward costs 4 to 38 times more" compared unequal work.** Held. *Changed:* item 6 times the same window forward with the read back's key filter. *Now:* forward costs what back costs; the fold's 7 to 41 times comes from decoding and writing the sibling's records.

## Verdict

**Most of what was owed is small, but less small than the first pass said, and three costs turn on record-format choices not yet made (DEV-09): the version's and cursor's text, the gap that finds a partition's earlier records, and keys first as bytes.** `c.version` and a diff entry's version sit at the 50 ns bar at epoch 0 and over it once a slot has moved, unless the epoch's digits are made once per slot. A one-partition `c.cursor` misses in both forms tried; a whole-resource one cannot be built per commit, so it is built only when read and read rarely. The `ended` guard is cheap (+2.6 to +15 ns a read), not free. A version kept in the row's own entry beats E-56's `Map` on time and bytes. An `equals` guard is cheap below about 1 KB, and a hash in its place is worse. Counting back costs about 0.4 ms at 10^6 partitions the first time, with a slot index and keys compared as bytes; a cold partition beside a hot one reads every slot record since, up to all of `history`; a gap to the partition's previous record, on top of the slot index, makes any count back 5 reads. A field's `-5` is cheap for any reader that skips the sibling's records on their key bytes, and dear for one that folds them through data.

## Limits

- **Prototypes, not fero.** Item 1 runs on E-55's prototype with a stand-in frame header made by the writer: no log, no frames, one record per commit. A real commit with several records gives each diff entry its own version from the frame, built the same way *(reasoned)*. Item 3's handle is a stand-in object; the guard was timed only while the link is live (the throwing path was only checked).
- **The log is in memory.** Items 2 and 6 build E-19's layout in one buffer of up to about 270 MB, with the slot index as plain arrays. There are no segments, snapshots, compaction, slot moves, page-cache misses, disk reads or object-store reads: a cold partition whose records sit on disk or in the store pays those on top *(reasoned)*. "Cold" here means not in the CPU's caches; the log is still in memory. The workloads (uniform partitions, one hot partition, cold partitions at chosen ages, one row with a 1 % share) are made up; the counts follow from them, the costs per record do not. Item 6's times are warm.
- **The version store is a scratch patch.** Only the packed lane of data's `Store` and `SourceNode.write` are changed; `lane()`, `remove` with versions kept, and compaction under load were not timed. Versions are doubles, exact while the epoch stays below 2^13 with 40 bits of sequence; a full 63-bit version needs two columns or a `BigUint64Array` *(reasoned)*.
- **The guard is the leader's check only.** Item 5 times the compare and the request's decode, not the frame around it, nor a write that follows. The hash forms use the value's JSON text, which depends on key order. The deep compare in a link (T11) was not run.
- **Noise.** Rounds swing by hundreds of ns (up to ±1,300 ns at 10^6 rows). Item 1's end-to-end numbers carry up to 30.7 ns of noise between two copies of one way, so its "alone" numbers are the cleaner ones; its version alone also pays a write barrier for keeping the text on an old object *(reasoned)*. Items 3 and 4 cannot resolve tens of ns where every read is a cache miss (10^5 and 10^6 rows). Item 3's +5 to +15 at 10^6 rows with one key, for every form of the flag, is not explained.
- **One box:** 4 CPUs, Node 22.22.0, Linux 6.18, data at `63fea66` ([results/versions.txt](results/versions.txt)). Other studies shared the box; every timed process held the timing lock, and every new line carries the CPU time others used while it ran. Items 4 and 5 ran on 2026-11-21 between about 11:10 and 12:35 UTC, before that check existed; items 1, 2, 3 and 6 were run again after the review, between about 14:30 and 16:01 UTC.

## How to run

```sh
SP=<your scratch folder> ./run.sh                 # clean results/, every bench three times under one hold of the timing lock, then results/summary.md
SP=<your scratch folder> ./run.sh quick           # everything once at small sizes, under the heavy lock, into $SP/e62/quick: a check that it runs
SP=<your scratch folder> ./run.sh countback ended # the named benches only, their old lines replaced
SP=<your scratch folder> ./run.sh again fields    # one more run of the named benches, appended, when summarize.mjs set a run aside
SP=<your scratch folder> ./run.sh summary         # results/summary.md from the raw lines
```

`SP` holds the lock helpers `bin/timing` (runs a command alone on the box) and `bin/heavy` (shares it, and waits for timing runs). `NODE` defaults to `node` on PATH (Node 22.22.0). `RUNS` defaults to `1 2 3`. `DATA` is data's folder (default `../data` next to this repo, read only, at `63fea66`); the version store's bench runs on a copy of its `kernel/` with [patch/data-ver.patch](patch/data-ver.patch) applied, made under `OUT` (default `$SP/e62`). `FLUSH_MB` sets the buffer countback reads to clear the caches (default 1,024). Every process runs with a 3,000 MB heap cap; the biggest needs about 2 GB. Nothing is downloaded.
