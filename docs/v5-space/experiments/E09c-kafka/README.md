# E-09c: Kafka 4.4 (KRaft, three nodes) on this box, with Kafka's own tools

> **C = 0.73 ms** (from [E-01](../E01-floors/README.md): fdatasync p99 with three writers 676 µs + Rust loopback round-trip p99 58 µs). The TS latency bar is C + 5 ms = 5.7 ms; the native bar is C + 0.5 ms = 1.2 ms.

## The question

Q-09, Kafka row (QUESTIONS.md §2, E-09c): what does Kafka reach here, with Kafka's own tools, at matched guarantees and at its default? It sets the bar numbers of PERF-05: 1 KiB records, 16 partitions, replication 3, `acks=all`, `min.insync.replicas=2`; the metrics are the **most MB/s** and the **p99 end-to-end latency at half of that rate**. Matched is `flush.messages=1` (fsync before the ack) against fero's `persisted`; the default (no fsync) is against fero's `memory` rung. Share groups (Kafka's queues, KIP-932) give the work-queue bar (P-09). Read against [E-02](../E02-pipeline/README.md)'s pipeline floors: can a TS build win these rows at all?

## The answer in short

All measured here, medians of 3 runs, each on a freshly started cluster. Ranges are in the tables below. MB/s is MiB/s, as Kafka's tools print it.

- **Default** (`acks=all`, no fsync: answered when every in-sync replica has the record in memory). With OMB's throughput settings Kafka takes **240,043 records/s = 234 MiB/s**, on 1.74 broker cores in all: **7.3 µs of broker CPU per record**. With the producer's own defaults (16 KiB batches) it takes only 65,807/s, at 41 µs per record: on this box the batch size, not the broker, sets Kafka's throughput.
- **Matched** (`flush.messages=1`: fsync after every append, before the ack). **86,344 records/s = 84 MiB/s** with OMB's settings (2.8 × below the default), 20.9 µs of broker CPU per record; 8,798/s with the producer's defaults (producer p50 2.8 s). Kafka's own docs advise against this setting ([results/cited/kafka-docs-flush.txt](results/cited/kafka-docs-flush.txt)); it is the only way Kafka offers fero's `persisted` guarantee.
- **Latency at half of max** (PERF-05's point): default, offered 120,021/s, end-to-end **p50 63 ms, p99 638 ms**; matched, offered 43,171/s, end-to-end **p50 75 ms, p99 869 ms**. At half of max Kafka is already queueing on this box: with OMB's latency settings (128 KiB batches) the same records cost 2.5 × the CPU of the max run, so brokers and producer keep 3.65 of the 4 cores busy. At OMB's standard 50,000/s the default gives p50 2 ms, p99 15 ms; matched p50 51 ms, p99 614 ms (50,000/s is 58 % of the matched max, past its knee).
- **One record in flight** (AckProbe, µs timer): default **ack p50 0.61 ms, p99 1.64 ms**; matched **ack p50 2.53 ms, p99 5.34 ms** (an fsync on each of three brokers plus the followers' fetch round trips, one after another). Through `kafka-e2e-latency` (ack, then consume; whole ms): default p50 1, p99 4 ms; matched p50 3, p99 8 ms.
- **Consumers.** One classic consumer reads 314,685 records/s over the whole run (522,042/s once the group has joined). **Share groups (Kafka's queues, live in 4.4):** over a whole run, 4 consumers in one new share group read 78,534 records/s at the default and 26,484/s matched. Most of that run is a wait, not reading: a new share consumer gets its first record only after **5.30 s (5.14-5.83)**, and then the 4 consumers drain 900,000 records at **232,258 records/s (200,915-261,399)** (per-second diagnostic, 3 runs under the timing lock). The wait is one share-group heartbeat, 5,000 ms by default with a floor of 5,000 ms (cited from Kafka 4.4.0's docs); with both set to 1,000 ms the wait is @@HBWAIT@@. After its own group join, one classic consumer still reads 2.2 × faster than the 4 share consumers (522,042/s). Details: [Share groups: the wait and the drain](#share-groups-the-wait-and-the-drain).
- **The OpenMessaging Benchmark itself was not run**: the brokers and one producer already keep 3.2-3.6 of the 4 cores busy, so OMB's extra worker JVMs do not fit; its exact producer settings and its end-to-end method are used instead.
- **Against E-02** (pipeline floors at 1 KiB on this disk, no protocol): the matched max is **3.3 × under the TS ceiling** (283,881/s), so TS is not stopped on PERF-05's matched row. The **default max is only 1.18 × under TS's `persisted` ceiling** (1.45 × under Rust's), and Kafka's broker CPU per record (7.3 µs, with the whole protocol) is about the TS pipeline's (6.6 µs, with none), so Kafka's default max row is at risk for a TS build and needs the `memory` rung, which E-02 did not measure at 1 KiB. Kafka's **p99 at half** is the easiest bar in E-09: E-02's TS floor has 19 ms at 141,941/s, against Kafka's 638 ms (default) and 869 ms (matched).

## Method

- **Kafka 4.4.0** (Scala 2.13, the Apache release, sha512 checked: [fetch.sh](fetch.sh)) on **OpenJDK 21.0.12.1**. **Three KRaft nodes, each a broker and a controller** (combined mode), on loopback ports, each with a **1 GB heap** (`-Xmx1G -Xms1G`) and its own log directory on the one ext4 disk. Every other broker setting is Kafka's shipped `server.properties` (3 network threads, 8 I/O threads, 1 GiB segments), plus: internal topics replicated 3 ways like the data, `group.initial.rebalance.delay.ms=0` (no 3 s wait before a group's first join) and `log.segment.delete.delay.ms=1000` (a deleted topic frees the disk at once). The driver writes the configs: [kbench.py](kbench.py).
- **Topics:** 16 partitions, replication 3, `min.insync.replicas=2`; the driver waits until all 16 partitions have 3 in-sync replicas before a test starts. Every record is 1,024 bytes, every producer uses `acks=all`. Note that `acks=all` waits for **every** in-sync replica (all 3 when healthy), not just 2; `min.insync.replicas=2` only says when Kafka refuses writes.
- **Two setups:**
  - `default`: Kafka as shipped. The ack comes when all in-sync replicas have the record in memory (the OS page cache); nothing is fsynced on the write path. Kafka relies on replication for durability. This is the **competitor's default** (SPACE's Q `maj-m`/`isr`: in memory at the replicas, not on disk).
  - `flush1`: the same plus the topic config `flush.messages=1`: each broker fsyncs a partition's log after every append, before it counts as written. This is the **matched** row (fero's `persisted`).
  - [results/fsynccheck.txt](results/fsynccheck.txt) proves the difference (not a timing run: strace slows the broker): at 2,000 records/s, one broker made **54 fsync calls in 8 s at `default`** (segment and checkpoint files only) and **1,666 at `flush1`** (about 208 a second, one per small append).
- **Each phase runs on a freshly formatted cluster**, after about 10 s of unrecorded full-speed producing that warms the JIT and caches ([results/warmcheck.txt](results/warmcheck.txt): a cold cluster streamed at about 3 × the p50 of a warm one).
- **Phase `max`** (in this order, on one cluster):
  - `max-default`: `kafka-producer-perf-test` at full speed with the **producer's own defaults** (16 KiB batches, `linger.ms` 5), 900,000 records at `default` (220,000 at `flush1`), the first sixth reported apart as warm-up (`--warmup-records`; the tables use the steady-state line).
  - `consume`: `kafka-consumer-perf-test` reads those records back in a new consumer group (the whole-run rate includes the group join, about 1 s; the "fetch" rate leaves it out).
  - `share-4c`: `kafka-share-consumer-perf-test` with **4 consumers in one share group** reads the same records (the group's `share.auto.offset.reset` is set to `earliest`). Share groups are Kafka's queues: records are handed out one by one to any consumer and acknowledged one by one, like a work queue.
- **Share-group diagnostic** (`sh run.sh sharediag` and `sh run.sh sharediag-hb`, each one timing hold on one fresh `default` cluster; [tools/sharediag.py](tools/sharediag.py)): after the usual warm-up, the producer writes 900,000 records with OMB's throughput settings, then `kafka-share-consumer-perf-test` (4 consumers, a new share group each time) reads them 3 times with a report every second (`--show-detailed-stats`), then one classic consumer. The tool prints a consumer's first report when its first record arrives, so that report's fetch time is the wait before the first record. [tools/sharediag_parse.py](tools/sharediag_parse.py) turns the reports into the wait, the drain rate after it (all records ÷ (end − the median first-record time)) and the whole-run rate. `sharediag-hb` is the same with the brokers' share-group heartbeat (`group.share.heartbeat.interval.ms` and `group.share.min.heartbeat.interval.ms`) at 1,000 ms instead of 5,000 ms; nothing else differs.
  - `max-omb`: the producer at full speed with the **OpenMessaging Benchmark's Kafka throughput settings** (`linger.ms=1`, `batch.size=1048576`; cited from OMB's driver config, [results/cited/](results/cited)), 2,500,000 records at `default` (1,500,000 at `flush1`). This is PERF-05's "max MB/s".
- **Phase `lat`** (on a fresh cluster):
  - `half`: the producer throttled to **half of `max-omb`'s median rate** (PERF-05's point), with OMB's latency settings (`linger.ms=1`, `batch.size=131072`), for 20-35 s (at most 2.5 million records); the first 5 s are warm-up. Beside it, [e2e/E2eConsumer.java](e2e/E2eConsumer.java) (Kafka's Java client, assigned every partition, no group) measures **end-to-end latency the way OMB does**: receive time minus the record's CreateTime, stamped by the producer at `send()`; 1 ms resolution. The producer's own send-to-ack latency is reported beside it.
  - `rate50k`: the same at **50,000 records/s**, the rate of OMB's standard workload `1-topic-16-partitions-1kb` (cited). It runs only if `max-omb` reached 55,000/s.
  - `e2e1`: `kafka-e2e-latency`, Kafka's own tool: one record at a time, produce with `acks=all`, wait until its consumer receives it, repeat; 12,000 records at `default` (6,000 at `flush1`). It prints whole milliseconds, rounded down.
  - `ack1`: [e2e/AckProbe.java](e2e/AckProbe.java), the produce half of `kafka-e2e-latency` with a microsecond timer (Kafka's Java client, `acks=all`, `linger.ms=0`, one record in flight): 20,000 records at `default` (12,000 at `flush1`) after 2,000 (1,200) warm-up records, at most 40 s. It lines up with NATS's and etcd's single-client rows in µs.
- **CPU:** each broker's user + system time from `/proc` over the test ("server cores" is the sum of the three), the tool's own CPU from the driver's child rusage ("client cores"), and the rest of the box ("other"). "Broker µs CPU/record" = server cores ÷ records/s.
- **Runs:** 3 per phase, each on a fresh cluster; median and range. Every timed hold ran under `$SP/bin/timing` (the box alone); the load average before each test is in the tables and in [results/runs.jsonl](results/runs.jsonl). The 1-minute loads of 3-6 before tests are the run's own brokers and earlier tests of the same hold; no other job ran during a hold.
- **The OpenMessaging Benchmark itself was not run.** It is built with Maven from GitHub source (the refused kind of build when E-09c started, [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md)), and it adds its own coordinator and worker JVMs. On this box the three brokers plus Kafka's one producer process already keep **3.2-3.6 of the 4 cores busy** at full speed (box-busy column of [results/runs.jsonl](results/runs.jsonl)), so OMB's workers would compete with the brokers it measures: it does not fit 4 vCPU well (measured CPU, reasoned conclusion). Instead, Kafka's own tools run with **OMB's exact producer settings and OMB's end-to-end method**, which give PERF-05's two metrics. Its standard tests run 15 minutes each; this study's timing rule is about 60 s a run.
- **Machine:** 4 vCPU Firecracker VM, 15 GB RAM, one virtio disk with ext4 without a journal ([E-01 env](../E01-floors/results/env.txt)). Kafka 4.4.0, OpenJDK 21.0.12.1, Python 3.13 driver.
- **Resumed after a container restart (2026-11-07).** The `default` hold had finished on 2026-11-06 (23:28-23:42); the `flush1` hold had started but recorded no run when the container stopped. It was run again from the start on 2026-11-07; nothing finished was redone ([results/timing-log.txt](results/timing-log.txt)). A second restart (2026-11-07, after 04:30) stopped the share-group diagnostic before it printed anything; it was run again at 04:49, and the heartbeat run was added after it.

## Against the floors (E-01, E-02)

E-02's floors at 1 KiB on the real disk, `persisted` (on disk at 2 of 3 before the commit), 1 group: TS 283,881 appends/s (277 MiB/s), Rust 348,928/s (341 MiB/s), Go 351,458/s (343 MiB/s); p99 at each build's own half rate: TS 19.45 ms (141,941/s), Rust 107 ms (174,464/s), Go 105 ms; p99 at 50,000/s: TS 4.34 ms, Rust 3.37 ms, Go 3.18 ms; CPU per append (leader + two followers) at max: TS 3.07 + 1.75 + 1.76 = 6.6 µs, Rust 1.96 + 1.41 + 1.44 = 4.8 µs. E-02 has no client, no protocol, no consumer and one group (Kafka here has 16 partitions): it is a ceiling that a real kernel behind a Kafka gateway can only approach. E-02 measured `memory` (in memory at a majority, no fsync wait) only at 100 B.

**fero's side is not measured here.** Every reading above compares Kafka with E-02's pipeline floors, which are ceilings, not fero. PERF-01 wins are decided when Kafka's same tools drive fero's Kafka gateway at speed ([E-45](../E45-drivers/README.md); its run so far, 6,959 records/s on the prototype's simulated host, was a plumbing check, not a speed run).

**Why Kafka's p99 at half is so high here.** OMB's latency settings cap a batch at 128 KiB and linger 1 ms; at 120,021/s over 16 partitions the batches are small, so the brokers spend 18.3 µs per record (2.5 × the max run's 7.3 µs) and, with the producer and the e2e consumer, the box is 3.65 of 4 cores busy (busy-cores column, [results/runs.jsonl](results/runs.jsonl)). Kafka at "half of max" is therefore near the CPU limit of this box, and its queues set the p99. On machines of its own Kafka would do far better; a fero build on this box would meet the same 4 cores. At 50,000/s (1.18 cores of brokers) Kafka's default p99 is 15 ms.

**Outside CPU, and one phase run twice.** The first `flush1` latency phase (2026-11-07 00:33-00:44) had about 1 core of CPU busy outside the brokers and tools from its `e2e1` run 2 on ("other cores" 0.95-1.14 against 0.12-0.23 before), from a process that did not take the timing lock. The whole phase was set aside in [results/superseded/](results/superseded) and run again from 02:03: its run 1 is clean (other 0.13-0.22); its run 2 was cut off in `ack1` when the background job that had waited for the lock hit its 2-hour limit, so run 2's three finished tests were set aside too, and runs 2 and 3 were run again as whole phases (`sh run.sh flush1-lat --first 2 --runs 2`). The `flush1` latency rows in the tables are these three clean runs. The set-aside runs agree with them (for example ack p50 2.46-2.79 ms against 2.50-2.57 ms). Every other Kafka run has "other" ≤ 0.31 cores; the steady 0.26-0.31 in `flush1`'s `max-default` runs is the kernel's own fsync work.

## Share groups: the wait and the drain

Measured here: [results/sharediag.txt](results/sharediag.txt) and [results/sharediag-hb1000.txt](results/sharediag-hb1000.txt), parsed by `python3 -I tools/sharediag_parse.py results/sharediag.txt results/sharediag-hb1000.txt` into [results/sharediag-summary.txt](results/sharediag-summary.txt). |---|---|---|---|---|
| 5,000 ms (Kafka's default) | 5.30 (5.14-5.83) | 232,258 (200,915-261,399) | 95,928 (84,874-101,124) | 0.77 (0.72-1.11) |

- **The wait is one heartbeat.** In every run the four consumers' first records arrive within 0.07 s of each other, about one heartbeat after they start. Kafka 4.4.0 gives share-group members a 5,000 ms heartbeat by default; a group may set its own `share.heartbeat.interval.ms`, but the broker's `group.share.min.heartbeat.interval.ms` (5,000 ms, a read-only broker setting) is its floor ([results/cited/kafka-docs-share-heartbeat.txt](results/cited/kafka-docs-share-heartbeat.txt), cited). @@HBREAD@@
- **The drain is the queue's real rate.** After the wait, the 4 consumers take, acknowledge and drain about 232,000 records/s. It rises from run to run on the same cluster (200,915, then 232,258, then 261,399/s), so the JVMs are still warming: the median is a fair figure for this box, not a ceiling.
- **The timed `share-4c` rows** read records written with the producer's defaults (16 KiB batches), not OMB's 1 MiB batches. Taking the same 5.30 s wait off their fetch times (11.22-12.43 s for 900,000 records at the default, 8.29-8.57 s for 220,000 matched) leaves about 146,000 records/s (127,000-152,000) at the default and about 73,000/s matched (reasoned, not measured). Whether the gap to 232,258/s is the smaller batches or the warm-up is not isolated.
- **For fero** (P-09 work queues; PERF-04's JetStream bench is P-09's named bar, and this is a second one): Kafka's queue hands a new worker its first record about one heartbeat after it joins (5 s unless the broker's floor is lowered), then drains about 232,000 × 1 KiB records/s with 4 workers on this box. fero's side is not measured here.

## What this box cannot show

- **One machine per broker.** In production each Kafka broker has its own disks, page cache and cores. Here three brokers and the load tools share 4 vCPU and one disk: at full speed the box is 80-90 % busy, so the `max` rows are bounded by this box, not by Kafka's design. Kafka's published numbers (on three large machines) are far higher; they are context only (PERF-01).
- **Three disks.** At `flush1` the three brokers' fsyncs queue on one disk (E-01: three writers cost 1.4 × one at p99), so the matched rows are pessimistic in absolute terms, for Kafka and for fero alike.
- **Real networks.** Loopback round trips are 58 µs at p99 (E-01); a real network adds a hop or two to every ack and every replica fetch. Kafka's followers pull with fetch requests, so its ack latency carries one extra fetch round trip that loopback hides.
- **The default's real guarantee.** Kafka's default keeps a write safe by having it in three machines' memory. On one box a power cut empties all three page caches at once, so here the `default` row is weaker than it is in production. The comparison stays fair only against a fero rung that makes the same promise (`memory`).
- **Long runs.** Each run lasts at most about 60 s and writes at most about 7.5 GB (three replicas); 15 GB of RAM absorbs much of that in the page cache. An hour-long run at `default` would be bound by the disk's write bandwidth.
- **Millisecond tools.** Kafka's tools print whole milliseconds; AckProbe gives µs for the single-record ack.

## Verdict

Kafka 4.4 on this box, with its own tools and OMB's settings, is a strong throughput competitor at its default and a weak one when matched. At its default (no fsync) it writes 234 MiB/s of 1 KiB records at 7.3 µs of broker CPU per record, only 1.18 × under E-02's TS `persisted` pipeline ceiling, which has no protocol at all: the thinnest throughput margin in E-09. So Kafka's default max row needs fero's `memory` rung, and likely native speed, to be won. Matched (fsync after every append, a setting Kafka's own docs advise against) it writes 84 MiB/s, 3.3 × under the TS ceiling, so TS is not stopped there. Its latency under load is poor on this box: p99 638 ms (default) and 869 ms (matched) at half of max, against E-02's TS 19 ms. With one record in flight its default ack has p50 0.61 ms (below C) and p99 1.64 ms; matched, p50 2.53 and p99 5.34 ms. Share groups (Kafka's queues) work in 4.4: once assigned, 4 consumers drain 232,258 records/s, but a new consumer waits one share-group heartbeat (5.3 s at the default) for its first record, so a whole 900,000-record run averages only 78,534/s. That gives P-09 a second work-queue bar beside PERF-04's JetStream, but fero has no share-group gateway yet, so the row is not comparable.

Q-09 (Kafka): Kafka 4.4.0 (KRaft, 3 nodes with 1 GB heaps; Kafka's own tools with OMB's settings, as OMB itself does not fit 4 vCPU beside three brokers) takes, at its default (`acks=all`, no fsync), 240,043 × 1 KiB records/s (234 MiB/s) at 7.3 µs of broker CPU per record, end-to-end p99 638 ms at half of max (120,021/s, with the box CPU-full) and 15 ms at 50,000/s, and a single-record ack p50 0.61 / p99 1.64 ms; matched (`flush.messages=1`) 86,344/s (84 MiB/s), p99 869 ms at half (43,171/s), ack p50 2.53 / p99 5.34 ms; share groups with 4 consumers drain 232,258/s at the default once assigned, after a wait of one share-group heartbeat (5.3 s by default) before a new consumer's first record (78,534/s over a whole run). The matched max is 3.3 × under E-02's TS 1 KiB `persisted` ceiling, so TS is not stopped on PERF-05's matched row; the default max is only 1.18 × under it (1.45 × under Rust's) at about the TS pipeline's CPU per record, and E-02 has no 1 KiB `memory` run; E-02's TS floor beats Kafka's p99 at half by 33 × (default) and 45 × (matched). Moves: candidates with only Q `maj-d` (MIN, RUST, SRV, LEAN, LANE, PD, SLOG, MIN-P, MIN-TS, RLOG, EMB-V8, SIDE) and OBJ down on PERF-05's default max row unless they add `maj-m` (+1 on DEV-01) or the user accepts the loss by name (E-45's F7, now with a number); MIN-TS down a little more there (no CPU margin), RUST and SRV up a little (1.45 ×); every candidate up on PERF-05's two p99-at-half rows; no candidate moves on PERF-05's matched max; ISR (`isr`) and DRAFT, CELL, SPLIT (`mix`) unchanged; the share-group work-queue row stays not comparable until fero's Kafka gateway speaks ShareFetch and ShareAcknowledge (E-45).
