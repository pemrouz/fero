# E-52: healing when each resource is its own cluster

**Question (Q-52).** With no `#meta` and no shared member list, does healing by each group's own leader stay safe and live, and which rules does it need?

**Threshold.** The design holds every property at every bound, and the rules the ablation keeps fall inside the records' ranges (`healing-self@per-resource` 14 to 16, `planner-hints@per-resource` 3 to 7, `meta-members@per-resource` 1 to 3) → the records stand, with their counts now model-checked. A broken property, a stuck state, or a count outside a range → the design or the record changes.

**In short.**

- **Healing by each group's own leader stays safe and live with no `#meta` and no shared member list, once the design adds four rules that no record has** (model-checked: 4 machines; up to two faults, or one fault with one leave or one hint, or two leaves, or two hints with no fault; up to 3,704,723 states, every run complete; one fault with two leaves (`ll2f`) checked for safety only, to 6,000,002 states). No safety property breaks; no state is stuck with a live majority; healing finishes once a replacement exists; two leaders that rank machines differently never churn; every hint, one or two, stale or not, keeps every property and takes effect; leadership is handed back, and the preferred member is re-picked when it leaves.
- **The four rules** (each model-checked): (1) **promote only a learner that answers the leader now.** E-16's "not gone" lets a learner that caught up and then crashed be promoted after a change of leader, and the committed entries end up on 2 of the 4 new voters (P2C: 13 steps on `lf1`, 14 on `h1`; one crash, no second fault). (2) **When the preferred member leaves the stored placement, a placed voter becomes preferred**; else no hand-back ever runs again (PR, 2 steps). (3) **Dial a spare the directory lists before using it**: links run only between members of a group, so a spare is found only through the directory (PE, 2 steps). (4) **A lead hint moves leadership itself** (LE, 1 step where `settle.ms` never passes), **and never to a draining or leaving machine** (LD, 2 steps).
- **Rules by ablation (DEV-10, E-16's procedure): 44 of 87 kept** (43 with a counterexample, 1 undecided); 48 mutants, 46 caught, 2 undecided, none survived (96%). For the three records asked about: **35 kept (34 with a counterexample) against 22 reasoned**: `healing-self@per-resource` 22 (21 decided; reasoned 15), `planner-hints@per-resource` 9 (reasoned 5), `meta-members@per-resource` 4 (2 distinct checks; reasoned 2). The hand-back and the re-pick of the preferred member add 3 to 5 rules that no record counts.
- **What the records get wrong**: rule 15 (store the pick) is 4 rules, and its stated reason has no counterexample (the churn without a stored placement comes from a leave, and "remove a retiring voter first" alone fixes it; the stored placement earns its place because the platform's place hints need it). The record's high-bound `add.cand` is needed at the pick (`pick.cand`, P9 in 21 steps), and needed alone at the add (P9 in 20) though undecided in the smallest model. `promote.caught`, which E-16 dropped, is needed (P2C). The stale-plan check on hints is **not needed**, with one hint or two; nor is "a hinted machine must be reachable", nor leaving the directory. "Never pick a retiring machine" is needed once two machines leave (P9 in 21 steps).
- **What the first version of this page got wrong** (a reviewer's findings, each checked and applied, section "What a skeptic's review changed"): it ran one leave per run, so it missed `pick.notret`; its leader reached every live machine, so the directory never mattered; its leader forgot a machine's "retiring" when the link dropped, which made a leave look like the reason for "answers now" (the real reason is P2C, with no leave at all); its churn caps equalled the design's own longest run; its preferred leader never moved; a transfer could cross a cut; and P2C was not stated. Its headline "the design must add one rule" stands, for a different reason, and three more join it.
- **How sure.** High that the design is safe and live at the complete bounds (every reachable state, model-checked). Medium for the counts: the greedy order decides which of two rules that cover each other is kept (section 3 names the pairs found); `f2` and `ll2f` are checked for the design and, to a state limit, for the smallest model, not rule by rule (on these bounds each rule would cost 10 to 20 CPU-minutes and about 7 GB); the smallest model is undecided on `lf1` (no counterexample in 5,000,001 states); with two hints and a leave (`lhh`) the smallest model needs Raft's "transfer only to a voter" back, which rests on a gap in the world (section 3); and the hint and preferred-leader counts depend on properties stated here (DEP-17 and DESIGN-V5 4.2 make them MUSTs; their exact wording is this model's). One group, 4 machines; Raft abstract (E-16's).

## Words used here

- **Healing**: a group's leader keeps three voters on live machines: it adds a replacement as a **learner** (a copy that follows the log but does not vote), lets it catch up, **promotes** it to voter, then removes the old voter (a **swap**). A **surplus** removal takes the fourth voter away when the gone one comes back.
- **Resource is its own cluster** (V5-2): each resource has its own Raft groups; each group's leader heals that group; there is no project-wide `#meta`, so no shared list of members and no shared "gone" mark.
- **Link**: two machines that share a group ping each other (`link-pings` rule 1). A machine outside the group (a **spare**) has no link to the group's leader until the leader **dials** it.
- **Gone**: a machine the leader's own link has not reached for `evict.after`. A link that is up is seen at once.
- **Soft directory** (V5-3): a table rebuilt from what servers announce. It keeps no log and decides nothing. An entry lives while its announcer's link to the directory lives, so a machine that died stays listed for a while.
- **Placement**: the machines a group's voters should be on, written as an entry in the group's own log (`healing-self@per-resource` rule 15). A **pick** is a leader choosing a machine for a new copy; picks are by load, so two leaders can choose differently.
- **Hints** (`planner-hints@per-resource`): asks from the platform to a group's leader: `gone(m)` (treat m as gone now), `lead(m)` (hand leadership to m and make it the preferred leader), `place(machines)` (store this placement), and `drain(m)` (sent to the process: hand over what you lead). A hint names the config it read; a stale one is refused (`stale-plan`).
- **Retiring**: a process that leaves (Ctrl-C) first says so in its pings, and then gets no new copies (`meta-members@per-resource`). **Draining**: a process told to hand over its leaderships before a restart.
- **Preferred leader** and **hand-back** (decision 21a, DESIGN-V5 4.2 "Leaders spread"; V5-7 set its pace): each group has a preferred member; once it has been back and caught up for `settle.ms`, the leader hands leadership back to it.
- **DEV-10**: a **rule** is one guard conjunct or one state update; it counts only if removing it lets the checker find a counterexample to a stated property (ablation). **Mutants** are made by script: drop each guard and update, flip each comparison, swap each pair of updates in one action.
- **AG EF**: "from every reachable state, the goal can still be reached", the liveness check of E-14, E-16 and E-33.
- **Model-checked** here means every reachable state of the stated bound was explored, breadth first, so every trace given is a shortest one. A run that **stops at the state limit** explored only that many states: no counterexample among them, nothing more.

- **E-16's world**, unchanged where not stated: [models/resheal.py](models/resheal.py) imports E-16's [models/heal.py](../E16-controller-loop/models/heal.py) and subclasses its `World`. ### The world: what changes from E-16's (not counted)

- **No `#meta`.** heal.py's `#meta` is pinned off: no `#meta` faults, no `#meta` observer, no option reads it.
- **No gone mark in a shared place.** A leader reads gone from its own links (heal.py's marks per side, which E-16's `self` already used: the observers on one side lose a link at the same moment), plus the gone hints it took itself. A hint lives in the leader's memory: it goes when the leader changes, and on any step where the leader's link to that machine comes up (a recovery, a healed cut, a dial, the machine joining the group), as heal.py drops a gone mark.
- **Links only between members of the group** (`link-pings` rule 1). The group's members (voters and learners, in the committed or the pending config) ping each other. A spare has no link to the leader until the leader dials it; a dialed link lasts until the leader changes or the machine is evicted. The leader "reaches" a machine only over a link, and only that link tells it what the machine says.
- **The soft directory** (V5-3). A set of listed machines. An up machine that is not retiring announces itself (a calm step). A machine that went down stays listed until a `time` step drops its entry; a machine cut off from the leader stays listed (it still reaches a seed on its side). **A spare is known to a leader only through its entry**, which can be stale. To use it, the leader **dials** it (`dial`, counted): the dial opens a link only if the machine is up on the leader's side. The leader dials while it looks for a machine (a voter is gone or left out of the stored placement, or a placed machine is gone or said it is retiring); when to dial is the leader's own business, and a dial at another time only adds a link (reasoned; it keeps the state count down).
- **What a leader heard.** Each side keeps the last thing it heard a machine say (draining, retiring) once it stops reaching that machine. So a machine that said "retiring" and then exited stays "retiring" to the leader. This model's first version forgot it when the link dropped; that reading is kept as the probes `resnow` and `resgonenow` (section 2).
- **Picks are by load, so two leaders can rank machines differently**: every choice of a machine (for a pick, an add, a promotion, a surplus removal, a hint's content) is a free choice. The rules alone must keep the choices safe.
- **The placement** is an entry in the group's log, `('place', machines)`. It commits as any entry does (heal.py's commit, a majority of voters) and then is the group's stored placement. The group's first placement is its founding voters (the genesis writes it).
- **Hints arrive at leaders** as outside steps (`control`, within a budget): `gone(m)`, `lead(m, config read)`, `place(machines, config read)` and `drain(m)` (to the process). A hint's content is free and may be stale (it names the config before the last change, or the current one): **a property must hold for any hint**.
- **A leave** (Ctrl-C, an outside step within a budget). The leaver exits (goes down for good) once it holds no voter seat and leads nothing. A process that stopped mid-drain or mid-leave starts fresh when it recovers. A draining process restarts as an outside step.
- **The preferred leader** is stored with the group; it starts as member 0. The hand-back: once the preferred member has been back as a caught-up voter for `settle.ms` (a `time` step), the leader may hand leadership to it. The pace (V5-7: a cap on transfers in flight, busiest groups first) is abstracted: any number in flight, any order. **When the preferred member leaves the stored placement, the leader makes the first voter of the stored placement preferred** (`repref`, counted): DESIGN-V5 4.2 makes the preferred leader "the full voter the ring ranks first", which moves as members leave; with no ring, the stored placement stands in.
- **A leadership transfer** is Raft's TimeoutNow. It reaches the target only if the target answers the leader; otherwise nothing happens (Raft's transfer times out). Then the target leads at once and commits an entry of its own term, as heal.py's election does. This model's first version let a target across a cut, or a dead one, take leadership.

### The design (counted): 87 rules written, 5 world steps pinned

Every rule is one guard conjunct or one state update in [models/resheal.py](models/resheal.py) (`ResHeal.actions`); `python3 ../E16-controller-loop/engine/cli.py models/resheal.py res --rules` lists them with their notes.

- **Healing**, E-16's five actions with E-16's names and guards (`add`, `promote`, `remove`, `surplus`, `dropl`; 38 rules), with four changes: a replacement is `need`ed while a voter is gone *or the stored placement leaves a voter out*; `surplus.last` removes *the voter the stored placement leaves out* (E-16: the one the ring ranks last), as the record says; two new guards, `add.placed` and `promote.placed`: add or promote only a machine the stored placement names; and **`promote.live` reads "the learner answers the leader now"** (E-16: "not gone"; section 2 shows why). `add.cand` reads "the leader's own link reaches it now", which for a spare means the leader dialed it.
- **Finding a spare** (`dial`, 2 rules): the leader dials a machine the directory lists, so that its own link can reach it (`dial.link`), and only a working leader does (`dial.leader`).
- **Rule 15, the pick** (`pick`, 7 rules): when a placed machine is gone to the leader or said it is retiring, the leader picks another machine (a free choice), and **writes the new placement into the group's log** (`pick.append`) before any add. Its guards: a working leader, one entry at a time, the placed machine is gone or retiring (`pick.need`), the new machine is one the leader's own link reached within `evict.after` (`pick.cand`: the record's high-bound `add.cand`; a group member, or a dialed machine not yet evicted), it is not already placed, and it is not retiring.
- **Retiring** (`meta-members@per-resource`): the leave's first step says retiring in the leaver's pings (`leave.mark`) and takes its machine entry out of the directory (`leave.unlist`); healing never picks, adds or promotes a machine that said it is retiring (`pick.notret`, `add.notret`, `promote.notret`).
- **Hints** (`planner-hints@per-resource`): `lead` and `place` are applied only if the config they read is current (`lead.stale`, `place.stale`: rule 1); a gone hint is taken only while the leader's own link to that machine is down (`gone.link`: rule 2); a place hint's new machines must be ones the leader reaches, dialing a listed one (`place.reach`: rule 3); a lead hint also sets the preferred leader (`lead.pref`: rule 4) and transfers leadership (`lead.transfer`); a draining process says so in its pings (`drain.mark`), hands over every group it leads (`handover.self`), and no hand-over, hand-back or lead hint goes to a machine that says it is draining or retiring (`handover.notdrn`, `handback.notdrn`, `lead.notdrn`: rule 5). Plus the hint path's own effects (`gone.mark`, `place.append`).
- **The hand-back**: to the preferred member, only if it is another voter, answers, is caught up, and has been so for `settle.ms` (`handback.*`), and its transfer. **Who is preferred** once the preferred member leaves the stored placement (`repref`, 4 rules): only a working leader, only then (`repref.need`), the first voter of the stored placement (`repref.first`), and the update.
- **Leadership transfers' own checks** (`raft-leader-transfer`'s record, not the three asked about): the target is another voter that answers and is caught up, and the transfer itself.
- **Raft's own guards** on every action: a working leader proposes; one entry at a time (etcd).

Pinned (the world's bookkeeping, written as updates, never dropped or mutated): the start of a leave and of a drain (what the process itself knows, which the properties read), and each hint's budget.

### Properties

| Id | Property |
|---|---|
| P2 | heal.py's: a removal never lowers the answering voters that hold the commit offset below three when they were three or more. |
| P2C | **A config change never leaves the committed entries on fewer than a majority of the new voters** (judged when the change commits). |
| P3 | heal.py's: 3 voters, or 4 between a swap's two steps. |
| P5 | AG EF: from every state with a live majority, a leader commits (no stuck state). |
| P8 | AG EF, **healing finishes once a replacement exists**: from every state with a live majority, calm steps reach a working leader, an odd voter count, and no voter that is unreachable or retiring while a live machine that is not retiring could replace it. |
| P9 | **No churn**: no more changes of config or placement without an outside event than **twice the design's own longest run on that bound**, and at least heal.py's 8 ([results/maxchg.txt](results/maxchg.txt)). So a counterexample makes more than twice the changes the design ever makes there. |
| P9L | The same for leadership transfers (hand-backs, hand-overs): twice the design's longest run, at least 4. |
| P7 | **A retiring machine gets no new copy**: no add or promotion targets a machine that is retiring, if the leader could know it: its link reaches the machine now, or the leader heard it say "retiring" (healing.py round 2's P7, as far as a leader can know; the probe `lf1p7` judges every target). |
| LD | **A drained or retiring machine is never handed leadership**: no hand-back, hand-over or lead hint moves leadership to it. |
| LH | **A hand-back never undoes a lead hint**: it never moves leadership away from the machine the last lead hint named (DESIGN-V5 4.2: "so the settle.ms hand-back does not undo it"), while that machine is still placed. |
| DR | AG EF: from every state where the leader is draining or retiring and another live, caught-up voter that is neither could lead, calm steps reach a working leader that is neither. |
| LB | AG EF, **the hand-back happens**: from every state where the preferred member could lead (a live, caught-up voter, neither draining nor leaving) and does not, calm steps reach it leading. Not judged on `hln`, where `settle.ms` never passes. |
| LE | AG EF, **a lead hint takes effect**: the same, for the machine the last lead hint named. On `hln` only the hint itself can move leadership. |
| PE | AG EF, **a place hint takes effect**: from every state where the last acknowledged place hint's machines are all up, reached and not leaving, and no pick has replaced it since, calm steps reach exactly those machines as the three voters. A place hint is acknowledged when its entry commits; if a new leader loses the entry first, the platform sends it again (outside the model). |
| PR | AG EF, **a preferred member that leaves is replaced**: from every state with a working leader whose preferred member is out of the stored placement, calm steps reach a preferred member that is a voter of the stored placement. Without it, once the preferred member leaves for good, no hand-back ever runs again and no property notices. |
| P8 on `hgn` | **A gone hint takes effect**: on the bound where `evict.after` never passes, P8 is judged once the leader holds a gone hint for every voter it cannot reach, so healing must finish on the hint alone ("leaders replace it now", DESIGN-V5 4.2). |
| SWAP | Reported, as in E-16: states with a config change pending from which no leader can commit again (a second fault during a swap). |

"A hint never breaks a property" is not a property of its own: every property above is checked on bounds with hints, whose content is free. LB, LE, PE, PR, P2C and the bounds `hgn` and `hln` were added because, without a property that asks a step to do something, DEV-10 drops that step's effect and then every check on it.

### The ablation (DEV-10), as E-16 ran it

E-16's `Ablation` with `VERIFY_PASS1`: the sweep is the eleven small bounds, smallest first; the verify bounds are `h1` and `lf1`. Same state limits as before: 900,000 on the sweep; 5,000,000 on the verify bounds (raised from 2,000,000, since the design alone now has 1,629,572 states on `lf1`). The big bounds `f2` and `ll2f` are not verify bounds: E-16's verify pass runs every rule the sweep drops on each verify bound, and the design alone has 3,704,723 states on `f2` and more than 6,000,000 on `ll2f`, so each run would take 10 to 20 CPU-minutes and about 7 GB, for about 45 rules. Instead the design and the smallest model are run on both (results 1 and 3).

**Four changes, all to how runs are read or skipped** ([models/finish.py](models/finish.py), applied as text edits to E-16's own source at load time):
1. a put-back trial reads the verify bounds in order and stops at the first that fails, as every other step of the ablation does (the decision reads only whether a run fails, so it is the same);
2. a trial whose only verify result is a stop at the state limit, with no counterexample, may be the put-back, and is recorded as undecided on that bound (as E-16 records `verify_undecided`);
3. a pinned step's row (the world's bookkeeping, never dropped, mutated or reported) is not run on the verify bounds;
4. nor is a pinned step dropped on top of the smallest model in the final passes and the rows: it is never a candidate to drop, and with a hint budget lifted one such run outlasted a 25-minute piece, so the ablation could not finish (new in this version).

Each cached verify result is also stored under both `full` keys, since on a verify bound `run1` stops at the first failure either way (engine.py: `stop = (not full) or bname in self.verify_map`). Nothing else differs from `cli.py --ablate`. [models/par.py](models/par.py) runs pass 1, the verify pass 1 and the mutants on 3 cores first and stores them in the ablation's own cache, keyed and stored exactly as `Ablation.run1` does. The cache key holds the mutation and the bound but not the model, so `run.sh` deletes the cache before step 5.

The churn caps changed meaning: P9 and P9L now allow twice the design's own longest run on each bound ([results/maxchg.txt](results/maxchg.txt)), so every churn counterexample below makes more than twice the changes the design ever makes there; on `lh1`, `h1` and `lf1` the caps are 16 (14 on `h1`) and 8.

### What a skeptic's review changed (2026-11-14)

A reviewer reran parts of this experiment and found that some results rested on choices in the world, not in the design. Each was checked against the model and the design docs before it was applied; every number on this page is from the model after these changes.

- **Two leaves** (F1; `ll2` in the sweep, `ll2f` for the design and the smallest model). With one leave per run, no bound could show that the pick must skip a retiring machine: with two Ctrl-C's, the leader re-picks back and forth between the two leavers for ever. DESIGN-V5 4.2 names the case ("two members leaving one after the other").
- **The leader keeps what a machine said** (F2). The first world forgot a machine's "retiring" the moment its link dropped, so a learner that said "retiring" and exited was promoted (the 10-step wedge). The design's own words are "a machine that *said* it is retiring". The first reading is kept as the probes `resnow` and `resgonenow`. The rule the first version proposed for the wedge, "answers now", turned out to be needed anyway, for P2C.
- **Links only between members of the group, and a dial** (F3). The first world let a leader reach every live machine on its side, so the soft directory only ever added dead or cut-off machines: the model never asked healing to find a spare the leader has no link to. Now it does, and the dial is a rule.
- **A lead hint must act now** (F4; `hln`). Time is order only, so the hand-back always fires in the end, and "set the preferred leader" looked like enough. With `settle.ms` that never passes, a lead hint must move leadership itself.
- **The preferred leader moves when it leaves** (F5; `repref`, property PR). It was member 0 for ever; once 0 left, no hand-back ever ran again, and no property noticed.
- **A transfer cannot cross a cut or reach a dead machine** (F6). The first world let the target lead on its own side across a cut. Covering pairs for the hand-back are now reported (section 3).
- **The churn caps have slack** (F7): twice the design's own longest run on each bound, not equal to it. Before, a "counterexample" could be one extra change, not a loop.
- **P2C** (F9), the first property the task named ("no commit loses its copies below a majority"), is now stated. It keeps `promote.caught`, and it showed that E-16's `promote.live` is too weak (section 2).
- **Also run**: two hints (F13; `hh0`, `lhh`, `hpl0`, `hd2`), `f2` for the shapes without a stored placement (F14), the smallest model on `f2` and `ll2f` (F16), and E-17's put-back step on `lhh`. The reasons, counts and traces on this page were rewritten to match the runs (F8, F10, F12, F15), and `run.sh` now deletes the ablation's outputs before it starts (F11).

### 2. What the model found (each a rule the design needs, or a gap)

- **A learner that caught up and then crashed must not be promoted** (P2C; [results/probe-heard.txt](results/probe-heard.txt)). E-16's `promote.live` asks only "not gone" (reached within `evict.after`). Take a leadership change that leaves one voter behind (a leave's hand-over, or a lead hint), then a learner that catches up and crashes: the leader still promotes it, and the promotion commits on the two voters that hold everything. The new config has four voters and the committed entries on two of them (13 steps on `lf1`, 14 on `h1`; one crash, no second fault). One more crash then stops the group. **"Promote only a learner that answers the leader now" removes it** (the design runs above: `h1` and `lf1` hold). This is the rule the first version of this page proposed for the leave wedge below; P2C needs it with one crash and no leave. The design text supports it (DESIGN-V5 4.2: "let its log reach the leader's commit offset, exactly": a learner that does not answer cannot show that), but E-16's text, and so the record, say "not gone". **+1 rule** against the record (E-16's ablation dropped `promote.live` in its weak form; here it is kept in its strong form: section 3).
- **The leave wedge of the first version is closed two ways** (SWAP on `lf1`, the probe `lf1swap`, 1.6 million states each). A learner says "retiring", exits, and is promoted while one voter is down; the new config then has no live majority. With both first readings (E-16's "not gone", and a leader that forgets a machine's "retiring" when its link drops) 336 of 219,777 pending states can never commit again (11 steps). Remembering what the machine said closes it with the existing `promote.notret` (0 stuck, `resgone`, but P2C still breaks); "answers now" closes it alone (0 stuck, `resnow`). The design has both.
- **"A retiring machine gets no new copy" holds for every target** (P7 judged on every target, `lf1p7`: the design holds, 1,629,572 states). In the first version it broke in 10 steps, the same trace as the wedge; the rule that closes it is "answers now", or the leader's memory of what it heard.
- **Two leaves need the pick to skip a retiring machine** (`ll2`). With two Ctrl-C's in a row the leader picks back and forth between the two leavers for ever without `pick.notret` (P9 in 21 steps, the probe [results/probe-links.txt](results/probe-links.txt) and pass 1). DESIGN-V5 4.2 names the case: "two members leaving one after the other".
- **A spare is found through the directory and must be dialed** (`dial.link`: PE in 2 steps on `hp0` without it). With links only between group members, a leader has no link to a spare until it dials one the directory lists; "reached within `evict.after`" (`pick.cand`) then also means "a machine I have a link to", and it is needed (P9 in 21 steps on `hg1`: the leader picks a spare it never reached, a gone hint for it lands, and the placement flips between it and the crashed voter for ever). With E-16's links, where the leader reaches every live machine on its side (`resopen`), neither the dial nor `pick.cand` breaks anything on the sweep. **Leaving the directory (`leave.unlist`) is still not needed for healing**, with either kind of link: a listed leaver that is a spare exits at once (it holds no seat), so a dial to it fails and the add needs a link; a leaver that is a member is heard over its member link.
- **The preferred member must be re-picked when it leaves** (PR). With the preferred leader fixed at member 0, once 0 left the placement no hand-back ever ran again, and no property noticed. DESIGN-V5 4.2 makes the preferred leader "the full voter the ring ranks first", which moves as members leave. With no ring, some rule must say who is preferred next: here, the first voter of the stored placement (`repref`). Without its effect PR breaks in 2 steps (pass 1: `hp0`).
- **A lead hint must move leadership itself** (`hln`, where `settle.ms` never passes). Without its transfer, a lead hint only sets the preferred leader and waits for the hand-back (LE in 1 step on `hln`). Once the hint transfers, it must never transfer to a draining or retiring machine (`lead.notdrn`: LD in 2 steps on `lh1`: leave 1, then lead to 1). The transfer itself cannot reach a dead or cut-off machine, so "the target answers" (`lead.live`) is Raft's, not the hint's.
- **Following a stored placement moves copies more than needed** (P9's longest run on `lf1`, 8 changes, [results/maxchg.txt](results/maxchg.txt)): voter 1 loses its disk and is evicted; the leader picks 3 and writes the placement; 1 comes back with a new disk and 0 leaves; the leader adds and promotes 3, then picks machine 1 again for 0, adds 1's new replica, removes its old one, promotes the new one and removes 0. Every leader agrees, so it ends; it is wasted moves, not churn. A pick is not undone when the machine it replaced returns (a policy choice, not a rule: reasoned).

### 4. Rule 15 as a design choice: is a stored placement needed?

Inside the design, every add follows the stored placement, so dropping the write alone leaves healing nothing to follow (P8, 1 step): DEV-10 must keep it, but that only says the design depends on it. So the same healing was also run with **no stored placement at all** (no pick action, no `placed` guards, no place hint; a replacement is needed while a voter is gone or says it is retiring; the preferred member is re-picked among the voters), in three shapes ([results/noplace.txt](results/noplace.txt), [results/noplace-f2.txt](results/noplace-f2.txt), model-checked):

| Shape | Which voter a surplus removal takes | Faults and hints only (`f1`, `four1`, `hg1`, `hgn`, `hl0`, `hd0`, `h1`, `f2`) | With leaves (`l1`, `ll2`, `lh1`, `lf1`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `free`: picks by load, nothing stored | any (each leader's own ranking) | hold (`f2`: 2,962,422 states) | **P9 breaks**: 23 steps on `l1` and `ll2`, 42 on `lh1` (`lf1`: no counterexample in 6,000,004 states, the limit) |
| `ring`: one order every leader computes | a retiring voter, else the highest id | hold (`f2`: 2,109,347 states) | hold (`lf1`: 625,505 states) |
| `retfirst`: picks by load, one fixed preference | a retiring voter, else any | hold (`f2`: 2,962,422 states) | hold (`lf1`: 798,849 states) |

`free`'s churn (on `l1`): member 0 leaves; the leader adds 3 and promotes it (four voters); its load ranking says "remove 1", a live voter that is not leaving; now 0 is still retiring, so it adds 1 back, promotes it, and its ranking says "remove 1" again; and so on, with no outside event between.

So:
- **The record's own reason for rule 15 is not where the counterexample is.** "A new leader could remove the voter the last one just added" happens (a returned voter, a different ranking), but it ends: three live voters, nothing gone, no further step (`free` holds on every bound with faults only, `f2` included, where E-16 found its own `surplus.last` churn with two faults). E-16's world had no counterexample for it either.
- **The churn comes from a leave**: with picks by load, nothing tells a surplus removal to take the leaver. One fixed preference ("a retiring voter first", `retfirst`) closes it without storing anything, and holds at every bound run, two leaves (`ll2`) included.
- **What a stored placement buys that `retfirst` does not**: the platform's `place` hint (DEP-17, a MUST) needs somewhere to live and a healing that follows it, and once healing follows a stored target, the leader must write its own picks there too (else the target stays stale: the pass 1 result). So in the base, with place hints, rule 15 earns its place; without place hints, `retfirst`'s one guard would do the same job (reasoned from these runs; place hints are not in `retfirst`).

## What the model leaves out

- **One group.** A resource with several groups runs this healing in each group, by that group's own leader, with nothing shared between them but the machines and the directory (V5-2). More groups multiply states, not the rules any one leader runs (reasoned). The home group's other records (settings, slot table, group records: `meta-catalog@per-resource`, `meta-directory@per-resource`) and slot moves are not here.
- **Gone marks and what was heard, per side, not per observer.** All observers on one side lose a link at the same moment, so they agree. A leader that just restarted has seen nobody gone and heard nothing yet; it can only wait longer, or (with nothing heard) add a machine whose "retiring" it missed, which the target's own refusal stops (reasoned; the refusal is not modelled, below).
- **Raft is E-16's abstract group**: elections, commits, catch-up and transfers are atomic steps, and "caught up" is the truth, not the leader's last report of it. E-14, E-14b and E-14c check etcd's Raft itself. Raft's random timers are "any voter that holds every committed entry may win", which also lets a draining or retiring member win an election; DR then asks that it hand over.
- **The hand-back's pace** (V5-7: a cap on transfers in flight, busiest groups first) is abstracted to "any number, any order": with one group it cannot matter. Whether the cap evens a restart in seconds is E-53's question (many groups, real time), not this model's.
- **Dials happen only while the leader looks for a machine.** A dial at another time only adds a link, so it can only let the leader hear more and reach more (reasoned); leaving those dials out keeps the state count down.
- **The leave.** DESIGN-V5's leave (promote a caught-up learner, then remove the leaver, bounded by `leave.ms`) is done here by healing itself, through the placement: the leaver is picked out of the placement, and a surplus removal takes it. Not modelled: `leave.ms` (a leave that cannot carry and exits anyway), `meta-members@per-resource`'s high-bound rule "a retiring voter is left to its own leave until `leave.ms`", and a leaving learner being dropped from the config before it exits (here it just exits).
- **The target's own refusal** (`meta-members@per-resource` rule 1: "from then refuses to become a new copy of anything") is not modelled apart: a config entry commits without the target's consent, so the refusal could only stop the data copy, which no property here measures. The record's low bound 1 ("the refusal at the target alone does it") is not tested.
- **The soft directory** is one table of machines; clients, lookups, seeds and re-hosting (its own record, `soft-directory`) are not here. What healing reads from it is the list of spare machines, which can be stale.
- **Hints**: at most one per run on the counted bounds; two per run only with no fault (`hh0`, `lhh`, `hpl0`, `hd2`). **Two hints with a fault are not run.** `place(null)` and "a retire with no replacement stays pending" (`planner-hints@per-resource`'s high bound) are not modelled; a planner's lease and the admin key are not rules here (two planners only waste moves; the key is `cluster-credentials@per-resource`'s).
- **A lead hint's preferred leader, and a re-picked one,** are set at once, not through a log entry that commits.
- **Time is order only**: `evict.after`, `settle.ms` and a directory entry's death are steps that always fire in the end, in any order. So the delay `settle.ms` itself can carry no counterexample here (it is not "flapping" that any property measures); `hln` and `hgn` test the two places where "now" matters.
- **Bounds**: 4 machines; up to 2 faults, or 1 fault with 1 leave or 1 hint, or 2 leaves with at most 1 fault (`ll2f` only to 6,000,000 states), or 2 hints with at most 1 leave and no fault. Groups start at three voters: no growth from one voter, no two-to-one step back, no log replicas.
- **A gap in the world, found by the first ablation.** When the leader's own link moves (the leader is cut off, then healed), the world drops no gone hint, though every link changed at once; it drops a hint only when the hinted machine's own link comes up. The only verdict that rests on it is `gone.leader` (Raft's own guard, outside the three records).
- **Transfers' own checks** (`voter`, `live`, `caught`, `notme` on hand-overs, lead hints and the hand-back) are Raft's (`raft-leader-transfer`, 6 rules in the study). This model has no P1 (one leader per term, no committed entry lost: E-14's job), so a transfer to a lagging member breaks nothing here, and their verdicts here say little; they are reported, not counted in the three records.

## Against the threshold

The threshold: "the design holds every property at every bound, and the rules the ablation keeps fall inside the records' ranges → the records stand. A broken property, a stuck state, or a count outside a range → the design or the record changes."

- **Every property holds at every bound: yes for the design as amended here; no for the design as the records state it.** With E-16's "not gone" for a promotion, P2C breaks with one crash (13 steps on `lf1`, 14 on `h1`); with no re-pick, PR breaks (2 steps); with no dial, PE breaks (2 steps); with no lead-hint transfer, LE breaks where `settle.ms` has not passed (1 step). With the four changes (5 rules), every safety property holds, P5, P8, DR, LB, LE, PE and PR hold everywhere (model-checked, up to 3,704,723 states), and the only stuck states are E-16's two-fault window on `f2` (SWAP, 2,885 states), as in E-16. The first version's leave wedge (168 states on `lf1`) is gone: "answers now" closes it, and so would a leader that remembers what a machine said.
- **Counts inside the ranges: no for all three.** `healing-self@per-resource` 22 (21 decided; 18 at the record's grain) against 14 to 16; `planner-hints@per-resource` 9 at DEV-10's grain against 3 to 7 (5 at the record's grain, but only 3 of its 5 listed rules); `meta-members@per-resource` 4 against 1 to 3 (2 distinct checks, inside).
- So, by the threshold, **the records change** (section 6), and the design takes four more changes. Healing by each group's own leader itself stays: no new stuck state from having no `#meta`, no churn between leaders that rank machines differently (with the stored placement), and every hint safe.
