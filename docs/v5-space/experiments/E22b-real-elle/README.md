# E-22b: E-22's histories re-checked with real Elle and real Knossos

**Question (Q-22, real-Elle addendum; [QUESTIONS.md](../../QUESTIONS.md) §10).** E-22 checked its client histories with two Go tools: Porcupine, and a Go port of Elle taken from PingCAP's tipocket, because the real Elle (Jepsen's checker, in Clojure) could not be downloaded then. Do the real tools give the same verdict on the same saved histories? The histories are E-22's own, kept in `$SP/e22v`: the 1,200 saga histories, the 26 self-test histories, and the per-partition register histories. Threshold (QUESTIONS.md): the same verdict on every history; any disagreement is traced to a checker bug or a history-export bug.

**Words.**
- A *history* is the record of what clients asked and what they were told.
- *Elle* reads a history of transactions and looks for cycles between them. *List-append* is its workload: each transaction appends numbers to lists or reads whole lists, so every read shows the order of the appends before it. *Real Elle* here is jepsen-io/elle 0.2.8. *The port* is tipocket v1.0.0's Go copy of an older Elle, which E-22 used.
- The anomalies, in plain words: *G0*, two units wrote two rows in opposite orders; *G1a*, a reader saw a write of a unit that failed; *G-single*, a reader saw half of a unit (a fractured read); *incompatible order*, two reads of one list cannot both be true, so an element was lost; *duplicate*, one element appears twice.
- *Read committed* forbids G0, G1a, G1b, G1c and lost or doubled elements: that is P-15's MUST. *Serializable* also forbids G-single and the other G2 cycles: P-15's SHOULD. *Strict serializable* adds order in real time: a unit that finished before another began must come first.
- *Linearizable* means every operation seems to happen at one instant between its call and its answer. *Porcupine* (Go) and *Knossos* (Jepsen's checker, Clojure, 0.3.16) both check that against a *model*: here E-22's model of one fero partition (rows, versions, conditions, ops).
- A *partition* is the unit of order (one row in the register workload). A *mutant* is a kernel bug E-22 planted on purpose, so its histories are known to be bad.
- *calm*, *faults*, *shuffle* are E-22's three modes: no forced faults; one forced leader change and one slot move aimed at a write in flight; the same plus the cross-partition shuffle.

## In short

- **Real Elle gives the port's verdict on every saga history that matters for P-15** (measured here, [results/elle.md](results/elle.md)): on the 900 histories of the `saga`, `owned` and `nolock` variants, both verdicts (read committed and serializable) agree on all 1,800. In `saga` and `owned`, G0 and G-single show up in exactly the same histories in both; in `nolock`, which is broken in every history by both, real Elle finds G0 in 2 more. So E-22's findings stand as real Elle sees them: the saga as API.md writes it has G0 in 16, 19 and 10 of 100 histories; with F3's two rules (`owned`) G0 is gone in all 300; fractured reads (G-single) stay in 72, 66 and 77 of 100.
- **They disagree on 33 of the 300 `undo` histories, and real Elle is the stricter one every time** (27 read-committed verdicts, 15 serializable ones; the port said valid, real Elle says not). Root cause, a checker difference proven by an ablation: when a committed append is never seen by any read, Elle 0.2.8 places it after the last element any read saw on that list and draws edges from that (`elle/list_append.clj` in the 0.2.8 jar, `previously-appended-element`); the port draws no edge for it (tipocket `list_append/utils.go` `previouslyAppendElement`). The undo variant erases other units' appends (E-22's F4), so committed appends go unseen and real Elle finds the G0 cycles they make. With the port's rule swapped into real Elle, all 2,400 verdicts agree ([results/elle-portrule.md](results/elle-portrule.md)). Real Elle's verdict is the right one: E-22's own check of the final read (each unit marked done appears once in every list it wrote) failed in all 300 `undo` seeds, for exactly this: a done unit's element missing ([E-22's elle-runs.txt](../E22-api-prototype/verify/results/elle-runs.txt)).
- **The 26 self-test histories agree** (measured here, [results/selftest.md](results/selftest.md)): real Elle gives the expected verdict and finds the named anomaly in 8 of 8; Knossos, running E-22's model ported to Clojure, agrees with Porcupine and the expected answer in 18 of 18.
- **Knossos gives Porcupine's verdict on every partition history E-22 saved: 185,959 of 185,959, with no unknowns** (measured here, [results/knossos-summary.md](results/knossos-summary.md)). So the "sample" became all of them. All 1,800 register-workload (lin-kv) partitions are linearizable in both. On the 2,800 partitions of the known-bad runs (E-22's seven mutant runs and the lin-kv run from before fix G1) both find the same 94 not linearizable and the same 2,706 linearizable: the planted bugs are caught by both. All 181,359 partitions of the other 93 history files (180,759 of at most 120 operations, and 600 of 677 to 902 operations in p09 and p22) are linearizable in both. With the lin-kv runs that is E-22's "183,159 partition histories, all linearizable", now confirmed by a second, independent search.
- **No history-export bug changed a verdict, but E-22's export format only works with the port.** It writes completions only (no `:invoke`, no times), and the self-test cases carry no process. Real Elle's history library refuses that (a second completion with no invoke), so this experiment adds the invokes (method below). With read committed and serializable Elle uses no order of time, so this cannot change a verdict; but it also means neither Elle can check order in real time, and real Elle still passes 4, 5 and 3 `undo` histories at read committed that E-22's own final-read check knows are broken.
- **Knossos is about 160 times slower than Porcupine** here: 1.16 hours against 26 seconds for the same 1,566,400 operations (2 cores, a loaded box; not a timing run). That was still cheap enough to check everything, in heavy holds of 25 minutes or less. Its cost follows how many operations overlap, not how long a history is: 1.7 ms per operation on the register workload, but 0.2 ms on the 600 largest partitions, where at most 4 operations overlap.

## What it changes

- **E-22's verdicts stand** (measured here). Every finding E-22 drew from its checkers holds under the real tools: F1 (the one register history Porcupine failed before fix G1: a refusal answered from the writer's uncommitted tail) and the planted mutants are caught by Knossos too; F3's G0 in the saga, and its removal by the two `owned` rules, are seen the same way by real Elle; fractured reads remain (P-15's SHOULD stays open, as E-22 said). No candidate moves on this.
- **The port under-reports when writes are lost** (measured here). On `undo`, real Elle finds G0 in 183 of 300 histories against the port's 5, because the port ignores committed appends no read saw. A v5 test harness that used the port would pass some histories that lose committed writes. So TEST-13's "Elle" should mean real Elle (jepsen-io/elle), run from the JVM or through elle-cli, not the tipocket port. Reasoned from the ablation.
- **TEST-13's history format must be followed literally** (measured here, reasoned for the fix). TEST-13 already says "invoke and complete"; E-22's Elle export had completions only and no times, which real Elle refuses and which leaves real-time order unchecked: Elle cannot tell that a unit finished before the final read began, so a committed append that vanished unseen can leave no cycle. The fix is in the exporter: one `:invoke` and one completion per operation, each with its process and time, so real Elle can also check strict serializability. E-22's per-partition register histories already had call and return times, and Knossos used them unchanged.
- **Knossos is cheap enough to run beside Porcupine** (measured here, not a timing run). At fero's partition sizes few operations overlap, so Knossos checked every partition E-22 saved in 1.16 hours on 2 cores. Porcupine stays the fast inner check (26 s for the same set); Knossos is a fit second opinion for a nightly sweep.

## Method

**Inputs, unchanged.** E-22's files in `$SP/e22v`: `edn/*.edn` (1,200 saga histories, written 17:51 to 17:52 and checked by the port at 17:52, before E-22 wrote its summary; `elle-raw.jsonl` holds the port's verdict per file); `hist/*.jsonl` (per-partition histories of every check and mode, and of the mutants); `lin-kv-calm-before.jsonl` (the run before fix G1); and E-22's Go build, whose `main.go`, `model.go` and `selftest.go` are byte-identical to E-22's committed `verify/go/` (checked by `cmp` in [run.sh](run.sh)). The `before-fixes` EDN files were overwritten by E-22, so E-22's "before" Elle rows cannot be re-checked.

**Real Elle.** [clj/elle_check.clj](clj/elle_check.clj) runs `elle.list-append/check` twice per history, with `{:consistency-models [:read-committed]}` and `{:consistency-models [:serializable]}`, as E-22 ran the port (verdict "valid" only when `:valid?` is `true`, as E-22 counted the port's unknowns as not valid). Two adapters, both in [clj/e22b/pairing.clj](clj/e22b/pairing.clj): an `:invoke` is added just before each completion, with the same process and transaction (reads with nil values), and `:f :txn`; an op with no process (the self-test cases) gets its own. With these two models Elle builds no realtime or process graph (`elle/txn.clj` `additional-graphs`), so where the invokes sit cannot change a verdict. The JVM runs with `-Djava.awt.headless=true` (Elle loads a plotting library that otherwise fails on a box with no display; no plots are drawn).

**The root-cause ablation.** `E22B_RULE=port` replaces Elle's `previously-appended-element` with the port's rule (an append no read saw has no known predecessor) through `alter-var-root`, and nothing else; then the same 1,200 histories run again. [clj/elle_explain.clj](clj/elle_explain.clj) prints Elle's own explanation of a cycle.

**Knossos.** Knossos needs the same model Porcupine used. [clj/e22b/fero_model.clj](clj/e22b/fero_model.clj) ports E-22's `verify/go/model.go` line by line (reads, writes with conditions and ops, refusals, touches, claims, writer-made entries). Go's model is nondeterministic (a touch without a ttl may or may not move a version) and Porcupine tracks a set of states; Knossos wants one deterministic model, so the Knossos model is that set of states, and a step is illegal only when no state accepts it. Numbers are made doubles, as Go's JSON makes them float64. [clj/knossos_check.clj](clj/knossos_check.clj) builds each history as E-22's `main.go` hands it to Porcupine: every operation its own client; times `int64(t × 1e6)`; a call before a return at equal times (Porcupine's ordering); a refused write is an `:ok` completion whose value says refused, so the model checks the refusal was legal (Knossos would drop a `:fail` op unchecked); a write whose client never heard back but which committed returns at 1e12 ms, as E-22 exported it. It runs `knossos.competition/analysis` (the linear and WGL searches racing, as Jepsen's checker does) with a 60 s limit per partition. Porcupine's verdict per partition comes from [go/e22b.go](go/e22b.go), an extra file in a copy of E-22's Go build that adds two commands (E-22 kept only per-file totals): `parts` (Porcupine on each partition, 20 s limit, as E-22) and `dumpself` (E-22's 26 self-test cases with both Go tools' verdicts). [compare.py](compare.py) joins the two by (file, seed, partition).

**How the Knossos sample was chosen, by time.** The pilot checked all 1,800 lin-kv partitions (75,675 operations) in 128 s: 1.7 ms per operation, against 0.009 ms for Porcupine. The other 93 history files hold 180,759 partitions of at most 120 operations (954,768 operations; the largest has 60) and 600 partitions of 677 to 902 operations (473,907 operations, in p09 and p22). At the pilot's rate the small band costs about 27 minutes plus a few ms per partition, which looked like three heavy-lock holds of 25 minutes or less, so all of it was queued, in three chunks, each to be cut at 25 minutes. The first chunk ran in file order; the other two hold the slower p08 and p17 partitions (up to 37 operations each, about 5 ms per operation) and ran in a seeded random order, so a cut would leave a random sample. No cut was needed: the three chunks took 23, 21 and 20 minutes, and all 180,759 partitions were checked. The 600 large partitions were first planned as a sample of 5 per file, on the guess that at the pilot's rate they would take hours. That step had not started (it was waiting for the lock) when a container restart stopped it on 2026-11-07. Before running it again, a probe of those partitions ([overlap.py](overlap.py), [results/overlap-big.md](results/overlap-big.md)) found that at most 4 operations are open at once in p09 and 2 in p22, and Knossos's cost grows with overlap, not length. So all 600 were queued instead, in a seeded random order, with 120 s each and the hold cut at 25 minutes, so that a cut would again leave a random sample. No cut was needed: all 600 took 80 s. In the end nothing was sampled: Knossos checked every partition.

**Locks and cost.** Every JVM and Go step ran under `$SP/bin/heavy`, each hold under 30 minutes (the longest 23): about 1 h 20 min of holds in all, most of it Knossos on 2 cores (1.16 hours of Knossos time, so about 2.5 CPU-hours, under twice E-22's 1.5). Nothing here is a timing run; the times are what a loaded box gave (load 1 to 16) and only size the work. Three operational notes, none with an effect on a result: `run.sh` was edited while a queued step was still reading it, so that step's shell stopped with a syntax error after its Knossos chunk had finished all 83,500 jobs; the container restart above lost only a step that had not begun, and nothing finished was run again; and two kinds of noise were kept out of the files (Knossos's INFO lines, "More than 1024 reachable models", are filtered from `results/`; dom-top, an Elle library, writes the classes it makes at run time to `clojure.compile.path`, now `$SP/e22b/classes`, not this folder).

## What this box and this method cannot show

- **These are E-22's simulation histories, checked again, not new runs.** A clean verdict says the histories hold, in E-22's model of the kernel; real sockets and disks are E-46's and later phases'.
- **No order in real time.** E-22's EDN has no times, so neither Elle can check strict serializability or see a unit that committed before a read began. That is the likely reason real Elle still passes 4, 5 and 3 `undo` histories at read committed that E-22's own final-read check fails: their lost appends were never read, so no read-committed cycle forms. With times, the final read's missed append plus real-time order would form a cycle that strict serializability forbids (reasoned; not run, since the times are not in the files).
- **Knossos checks the search, not the model.** Knossos and Porcupine run the same model (ported by hand); a wrong rule in the model would pass both. The port of the model was checked on the 18 self-test cases, and on the 94 known-bad partitions both catch.
- **Linearizability per partition only.** As in E-22, each partition is checked alone; order and atomicity across partitions are P-15's, checked by Elle above.
- **Agreement is not proof.** Two independent searches agreeing (Porcupine and Knossos; the port and real Elle where they agree) makes a bug in either one unlikely to hide a verdict; it says nothing about a rule both were given (the bullet above). Reasoned.
- **E-22's eighth mutant run left no history.** Under `lostcommit` a client waits forever for its lost write, which stops the run: core exactly-once at its first seed (so `mut-lostcommit-core-exactly-once.jsonl` is empty), lin-kv and P-11 after 6 and 29 seeds. So the known-bad set is the seven runs with histories, as E-22's own mutant table shows.
- **The `before-fixes` saga histories no longer exist** (E-22's run step deletes the EDN folder), so E-22's "before" Elle rows are the port's only.
