# split: groups that split under load and merge back

**What it is.** The candidate SPLIT (SPACE P `split`, O `off+lin`; DESIGN-V5 decision 20a): a group splits when it gets big or busy, and later merges back when load falls. The shape is CockroachDB's ranges ([distribution layer](https://www.cockroachlabs.com/docs/stable/architecture/distribution-layer); its range split trigger and its merge with a Subsume request). Bigtable, HBase, TiKV, Spanner and Scylla tablets split the same way.

**The model:** [model.py](model.py). A bound's layouts read as HARNESS.md says: `grow` is a split of G0 that hands p's half to the new group G1; `shrink` is the merge of G1 back into G0 (p starts in G1, split off earlier); `twice` is a split and then the merge back.

- **Split** (grow). The group A that holds p commits **one entry**, `split(p → B)`. It is the decision and the fence at once: from the moment it is in A's log, A takes no write to p. Once it commits, B starts with an `init` entry holding p's state at the split: its rows, its dedup outcomes, its highest lock token, and the lineage base (p's last position in A). In CockroachDB the right-hand side is created on the same replicas by the split trigger, so nothing crosses the network; in the model B reads A's committed log up to the split entry, which is the same state. B serves p once its init commits. A split cannot be given up: it is one entry, and before it commits a crash simply loses it.
- **Merge** (shrink). The group B taking p back (the left-hand side) commits `merging`. The group A that holds p (the right-hand side) then commits `subsumed`, and from that moment takes no write to p. Once that commits, B commits `merged` with p's state (taken from A's committed log up to `subsumed`) and serves p. B alone may give the merge up (`merge-abort`, a timer, before `merged`); A then unfreezes on B's committed abort. One decider, as in CockroachDB, where the merge transaction decides and the frozen right-hand side waits for its outcome.
- **Positions** (SPACE O `off+lin`) are `(generation, group, log index)`. The generation is CockroachDB's range-descriptor generation: a split gives both sides the parent's generation + 1, a merge gives the merged group the larger of the two, + 1. Without it, a partition's positions fall when it comes back to a group with lower offsets (P2).
- **Records do not move.** They stay in the log of the group that wrote them, and the old group's log is kept for readers. **The reader follows lineage:** a record that comes after an `init` or `merged` entry may be taken only once the cursor has reached that entry's base. That is SPACE's "lineage resolution, kept forever".
- **The carried state is taken at the split (or freeze) point,** A's committed log up to that entry, as the triggers do. So a second `init` or `merged` for the same split or merge is the same entry, and the harness ignores it: no "once" guard is needed. (Slot's model copies A's whole committed log at the time of the copy, so its final copy needs a "once" guard. Part 2 tests that modelling choice on the lanes model, `lanes-atfence`.)

**Left out, on purpose:**
- Moving the new group's replicas to other servers after a split, and putting the two sides' replicas on the same servers before a merge (CockroachDB's "colocation"). Those are membership changes of abstract groups (E-14), and they are where a split's and a merge's data is really copied.
- Reads of state during a split or merge (CockroachDB's latches and lease handover). The harness has a record reader only.
- Splits of a split, and two changes at once. The bounds have at most two changes, one after the other.
- Unclean recovery, as for every option here.

**Results** (model-checked; [../../results/split-ablation.json](../../results/split-ablation.json); every rule with its trace in [../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/results/summary.md](../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/results/summary.md); part 2's README is [../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/README.md](../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/README.md)):
- **33 of the 47 rules are necessary**: fixN's 13-rule client path, plus **20**. The reverse greedy order (`split-rev`) keeps exactly the same 33.
- **The 20, split by protocol:**
  - **the split (9):** A takes no write once its split entry is in its log (`write.nosplit`); B starts only from A's committed split (`init.splcom`); B's init carries the rows, the dedup outcomes, the highest lock token and the lineage base (`init.rows`, `.outs`, `.tok`, `.base`); the reader takes a record after an init only once its cursor reached the base (`read.lin`); and the two appends (`split.append`, `init.append`);
  - **the merge (11):** A takes no write once subsumed is in its log (`write.nosub`); A freezes only for B's committed `merging` (`subsume.mrgcom`; else A freezes for a merge a crash then loses, and stays frozen: L5) and only a partition its committed log holds (`subsume.open`; else, after a split and a merge back, A freezes before its init commits, the init then reopens it, and two groups hold p: P1 in 10 steps); B merges only from A's committed subsumed (`merged.subcom`); B carries the rows, the outcomes and the token (`merged.rows`, `.outs`, `.tok`); the merged group takes a new generation (`merged.gen`; else p's positions fall when it comes back: P2); and the three appends.
- **The merge is a second protocol, and it repeats the carry.** The rows, the outcomes and the token are carried twice (6 rules), and each direction has its own fence and its own commit wait. Slot moves carry once, with one protocol for both directions.
- **Lineage costs 2 rules on the shared bounds and 3 on a bigger one.** On the shared bounds the reader's lineage needs `read.lin` and `init.base`. No shared bound has a reader crossing a merge with records on both sides, so `merged.base` is dropped. On `readback` (grow then shrink with the reader and two writes; [../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/extra.py](../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/extra.py)) the smallest set fails P4 in 15 steps and needs `merged.base` back: **34 rules**.
- **Not needed:** `split.open` (A splits only what it holds), both generations of a split (`split.gen`, `init.gen`: the group number already orders a split's positions; only a merge back needs a new generation), one merge at a time (`merge.one`), and the name check on `merged` (`merged.mrg`).
- **Mutants:** 59; 46 caught, 13 equivalent (each swaps two field updates of one entry), none survived.
- **The give-up path, priced** (`split+giveup`, [../../results/split+giveup-ablation.json](../../results/split+giveup-ablation.json)): B's `merge-abort` pinned. **39 rules: the core 33, the pinned abort, and 5 rules, each with a counterexample, none undecided.** Give up or finish, never both (`merged.noab`, `mabort.nomgd`; P6 in 10 steps without either); hand back only on a **committed** abort (`unsub.abcom`, P6 in 10 steps), only for a merge A froze for (`unsub.sub`; else the unfreeze lands before the freeze and A stays frozen: L7), with an entry that says so (`unsub.append`). The same five as a one-decider slot move (`slot+abort`), but no `importing` entry is needed: the merge's name is the decider's own `merging` entry. `twice1` holds (531 states).
- **States:** as written, 234 (`grow`) to 1,616 (`shrink`) states, about 100 times fewer than slot's 22,797: the split has two entries and no bulk copy or give-up branches. The smallest model has 72 to 540; with the give-up path, at most 1,672.
