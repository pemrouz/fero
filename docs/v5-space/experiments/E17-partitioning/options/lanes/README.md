# lanes: fixed project lanes, with key-range moves between them

**What it is.** The candidate LANE (SPACE G `lane`, P `fixN (lanes)`, O `off`; DESIGN-V5 decision 20b; V5-UNITS option 2): a project has a fixed number of Raft groups (64 by default), and every resource's partitions hash into them. Going past the lane count, or rebalancing, moves a key range from one lane to another: "going past the lane count means moving key ranges between lanes (Vitess's cut-over), in phase 5", with cursors as "per-lane offset; lineage in phase 5". This model checks that phase-5 machinery now.

**The model:** [model.py](model.py). p is a partition of resource P and q one of resource Q, and both start in lane L0: two resources sharing a lane (HARNESS.md). A range is a stretch of the project's one hash space, so it holds keys of every resource that hash into it; p is in range r and q in range u. The bound's layouts become moves of r between the two lanes (`grow` L0 → L1, `shrink` L1 → L0, `twice` there and back).

- **The range move is the slot move's protocol** ([../slot/model.py](../slot/model.py)), because LANE's control plane is the draft's (`self+p`: leaders run moves): A commits `exporting`; B commits `importing` and a bulk copy; on B's ready report A fences at N; once the fence commits, B takes the final copy with `END(N)` and the carried state, and opens. A may cancel before its fence; B may abort after it; A unfences on B's committed abort. Run instead by a catalog, as Vitess runs its cut-over, the move has re-key's shape, which part 1 priced.
- **Positions are per-lane offsets** (O `off`): `(epoch, lane, log index)`. An offset alone falls when a range comes back to a lane with a shorter log, so each range carries an **epoch**, raised by every move (the final copy carries it). That is the lineage generation.
- **Records do not move.** The copy carries the range's rows, dedup outcomes and highest lock token, not its records; those stay in the lane that wrote them. **The reader follows lineage:** a record after a move's final copy may be taken only once the cursor has reached that copy's `END(N)`, the range's last position in the lane it came from.
- **Variant `lanes-carry`:** the copy also carries each record with its original position (as slot's copy and `rekey-carry`'s do), and a lane serves those too, so no lineage rule exists.
- **Variant `lanes-atfence`** (a probe of a modelling choice, not a design): the final copy reads A's committed log up to the fence entry (the state at the fence) instead of A's whole committed log, as slot's model does. It asks whether slot's "one final copy" guard is a rule of the move or of how the model times the copy.
- The lane count never changes, so no group is created or deleted.

**Left out, on purpose:**
- What lanes cost outside the move: the ceiling of 64 writers per project, `copies` and durability made project-wide, one lane's leader shared by every resource. These are SC-03, SC-04 and SC-10 questions (E-18 counts the effective writers), not rules.
- Range boundaries chosen at move time (a range split inside a lane), and two moves of overlapping ranges. Here a range holds one partition and one move runs at a time.
- Everything slot's model leaves out (the routing table in `#meta`, unclean recovery).

**Results** (model-checked; [../../results/lanes-ablation.json](../../results/lanes-ablation.json); every rule with its trace in [../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/results/summary.md](../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/results/summary.md); part 2's README is [../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/README.md](../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/README.md)):
- **28 of the 68 rules are necessary**: fixN's 13, plus 15. That is **slot's 26 plus 2**, rule for rule:
  - the same move as slot's smallest move: A commits `exporting` (only for a range its committed log holds: `export.open`) and the fence; A takes no write once its fence is in its log (`write.nofence`); B takes the final copy only from a committed fence (`final.fencom`), once (`final.once`), with the rows, the dedup outcomes, the highest token and `END(N)`; B opens;
  - **positions:** slot carries the slot's sequence (`final.seq`); lanes carry the range's epoch (`final.ep`) instead. Without it a range that comes back to a lane with a shorter log gets lower positions: P2 in 12 steps on `shrink`. One rule either way;
  - **lineage, the 2 extra rules:** the reader takes a record after a move's final copy only once its cursor reached that copy's `END(N)` (`read.lin`), and N must be the range's last position in A's log (`fence.N`). Without either, the reader takes the first record in the new lane before the last one in the old lane: P4 in 13 steps on `read`. In slot's model the records travel with the copy, so a cursor needs nothing and `fence.N` is only a twin of `final.seq`.
- **Not needed by any property:** the same rules slot does not need (`importing`, the bulk copy and the ready report, the whole give-up path, the idempotence guards other than `final.once`).
- **Mutants:** 50; 39 caught, 11 equivalent (each swaps two field updates of one entry), none survived.
- **Reverse greedy order** (`lanes-rev`): exactly the same 28. Here `fence.N` and the carried epoch are not twins: N is the lineage base, the epoch orders positions.
- **Records carried** (`lanes-carry`, [../../results/lanes-carry-ablation.json](../../results/lanes-carry-ablation.json), warm-started from lanes; every bound held): **26 rules, slot's 26 rule for rule**, with the epoch (`final.ep`) in place of the sequence (`final.seq`). The two lineage rules go. Mutants: 46; 35 caught, 11 equivalent, none survived.
- **A bigger bound** (`readback`; [../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/results/extra.txt](../../../E17b-partitioning-part2/results/extra.txt)): lanes' set, in both orders, fails L7 in 17 steps and needs "fence a move once" (`fence.once`) back: after a move and a move back, a stale fence of the first move, with a new N, closes the range again. So **29**. `lanes-carry` holds (26).
- **The give-up path, one decider** (`lanes+abort`, [../../results/lanes+abort-ablation.json](../../results/lanes+abort-ablation.json)): B's `abort` and `importing` pinned, warm-started as part 1's `slot+abort` was. **37 kept: the core 28, the 2 pinned, 6 give-up rules and 1 undecided** (`abort.imp`, as in `slot+abort`). The 6 are slot's 5 and "fence a move once" (`fence.once`: after B aborts and A reopens, A can fence the same move again with a new N, and the move is stuck: L7 in 12 steps). So **36 to 37**, against slot's 33 to 34.
- **The copy's timing** (`lanes-atfence`, a probe): with the final copy read at the fence, lanes' smallest set without "one final copy" (`final.once`) holds every shared bound and `twice1`; as ablated, without it, it fails P1 on `both` in 14 steps. So `final.once` is a rule of when the model takes the copy, not of the move (27 rules with the copy taken at the fence).
- **States:** as written, the same as slot's at every bound (22,797 on `grow`): the protocol is slot's. Smallest model: 1,086 (`grow`) to 5,036 (`deep`); `twice1` 660. With the give-up path: 94,242 to 331,073.
