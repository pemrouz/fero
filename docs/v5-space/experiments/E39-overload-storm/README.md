# E-39: twice capacity, and a reconnect storm

**Question (Q-39).** What happens at twice capacity, and in a reconnect storm? Overload: clients offer more writes than the replicated pipeline can commit. Does it keep committing near its peak (goodput: the writes that commit while their client still waits), or does it fall into a "metastable" failure, where retries and timed-out work keep it overloaded even after the load drops (Bronson et al., HotOS'21)? Storm: a server holding 10,000 subscribers dies and restarts, and every client reconnects at once.

**Decides.** F-19, F-13, X17; admission control as a mechanism (DEV-01); the client's retry rules, which are API shape.

**Threshold (QUESTIONS.md).** Goodput ≥ 80 % of peak at 2 × and full resubscription ≤ 10 s with p99 ≤ 2 × steady → bounded queues plus retry-after are enough. Otherwise admission control (or server-side cursor tokens for resubscription) is priced now as a mechanism. F-19 adds: admitted writes keep p99 ≤ 2 × steady at 2 ×, and within 10 s of load falling to 50 %, p99 is back to steady.

## Method

### (a) The pipeline at 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 × its maximum

Code: [pipe/leader.ts](pipe/leader.ts) (TS leader), [rs/src/bin/leader.rs](rs/src/bin/leader.rs) (Rust leader), [rs/src/bin/load.rs](rs/src/bin/load.rs) (clients), [pipe/bench.mjs](pipe/bench.mjs) (driver).

- **The pipeline is E-02's.** One leader and two followers, three OS processes, each with its own journal on the real disk (preallocated 512 MiB segments, reused as a ring; E-02 found preallocation 1.3-1.9 × faster than growing files); a 1 ms batch window, one `writev` + `fdatasync` per flush (group commit), commit at a majority. The followers are E-02's own follower processes, unchanged ([ts/pipeline.ts](../E02-pipeline/ts/pipeline.ts) and E-02's Rust binary). The leader is E-02's leader logic with a client front: writes (100 B) arrive from clients over TCP instead of being generated in-process, and each client gets an answer per write: committed, or refused with a retry-after.
- **Two leader policies.** `unbounded`: every write is admitted; at most 32 MiB ride in flight (E-02's max mode) and the rest wait in an unbounded queue. `bounded`: a write is refused at once when admitting it would put more than `limit` writes between admission and commit; `limit` = 5 ms of the maximum rate (a queue bound in time, as CoDel sets one); retry-after = the time the backlog needs to drain to half the limit at the commit rate of the last 100 ms.
- **The clients** (Rust, two processes, two links each, open loop: arrivals do not wait for answers). New writes arrive at the offered rate, in 1 ms ticks. An attempt with no answer after 200 ms is abandoned (a late commit of it is wasted work) and the write is retried; a write not committed 2 s after it arrived is given up. Three client rules: `naive` retries at the next tick; `jitter` honours retry-after and waits max(retry-after, a full-jitter exponential backoff in [0, min(1 s, 10 ms × 2^attempt))); `credit` is `jitter` plus a client-side window of `limit` outstanding writes, split over the processes (writes beyond it wait in the client, where their wait counts in the end-to-end latency, not the admitted latency).
- **Combinations.** `un-naive` (unbounded leader, naive clients: no protection at all), `b-naive` (bounded queue and refusals, clients that ignore retry-after), `b-jitter` (bounded queue + retry-after + jittered backoff: what the question proposes), `b-jitter-t2` (the same with patient clients: timeout 2 s, deadline 4 s), `b-prio` (`b-jitter` with a leader that serves its commit path first: in TS it pauses reading the client links once they have handed over 1.25 ms of capacity in a 1 ms tick; in Rust the client readers hand over through a bounded queue and each loop takes acks and fsync completions first, then requests for at most 500 µs), `b-credit` (`b-jitter` plus the client window: admission control priced as a mechanism). Runs at 0.5 × only for `b-jitter` (the steady reference) and `b-credit`.
- **Maximum.** A closed loop (the clients keep 20,000 writes outstanding, no retries) for 12 s; its goodput is the maximum, per implementation.
- **One run.** Phase 1: 12 s at m × max (m = 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2), the first 3 s warm-up; phase 2: 8 s at 0.5 × max, to see whether the tail recovers. Every 500 ms the clients record new writes, good commits, wasted commits, refusals, retries, timeouts, give-ups and the admitted p99 (send of the attempt that committed → commit). Steady = the same combination at 0.5 ×.

### (b) The reconnect storm

Code: [storm/storm.mjs](storm/storm.mjs), with E-27's [server.mjs](../E27-idle-links/server.mjs) and [client.mjs](../E27-idle-links/client.mjs).

- 10,000 subscribers (two client processes) in 1,000 rooms of 10; each room publishes 1 message a second (10,000 deliveries a second). Each room's sequence is a function of time from a shared epoch, and a restarted server starts holding each room's last 64 messages, as if recovered from its log; a client resubscribes with its cursor (the last sequence it saw) and gets what it missed, then live messages.
- Steady 10 s (delivery latency, server CPU) → `SIGKILL` the server → start a new server process on the same port at once → the storm, until every link has resubscribed (at most 60 s) → 10 s after.
- Variants: plain TCP and TLS 1.3; clients that redial every 20 ms (`fixed`, no backoff) or with full-jitter exponential backoff in [0, min(3 s, 50 ms × 2^attempt)] (`jitter`); for TLS, with and without session resumption (both servers share session-ticket keys and clients offer their last ticket; without it every reconnect is a full handshake).
- Reported: time from the kill to 50 %, 99 % and 100 % resubscribed; delivery p99 in steady, in the storm (the first 10 s after the kill, live messages only) and after; the new server's CPU and bytes in its first 10 s; full handshakes against resumptions; catch-up messages; shortfalls (a cursor older than the history kept).

**Timing.** All of it ran under the timing lock: one hold per implementation and combination, one per storm link type; 3 runs each; load average recorded before each run. Medians with the lowest and highest run in brackets.

Reproduce: `../E02-pipeline/run.sh build` (E-02's Rust follower), `./run.sh build`, then `./run.sh all` (or the holds one by one: `./run.sh ts-max`, `./run.sh ts-b-jitter`, …, `./run.sh storm-tls`), and `./run.sh summary`. Machine: 4 vCPU, 15 GB, one ext4 virtual disk, Linux 6.18 (Firecracker VM), Node 22.22.0, Rust 1.97.0. Raw lines: [results/](results). Nothing was downloaded.

(5) The first three Rust `b-jitter-t2` runs at 2 × stopped when the client's ring of live writes (8 M slots) overflowed (a harness limit: 5.2 M writes a second times a 4 s deadline); the ring was doubled and those runs re-run. (4) The two load processes have two cores between them; once retries start, they saturate (about 2 cores), which a real client fleet on many machines would not, so part of the persistence of a collapse below sits in the clients. ### What the numbers say: the pipeline

1. **The maxima.** TS: 1.53 million writes a second (closed loop, 20,000 outstanding), CPU-bound in the leader (1.05 cores: parsing, batching, answering). Rust: 2.62 million, with the leader at 0.66 of a core: the one disk and the window bound it. Steady (0.5 ×): admitted p99 16 ms (TS), 10 ms (Rust).
2. **Without a client-side window, every combination collapses at or above 1 ×, and stays collapsed after the load falls to 0.5 ×** (goodput 0-50 % of max at 1 ×, about 0 at 1.5 × and 2 ×, phase-2 goodput about 0 for TS). This holds for the unbounded leader with naive clients, for the bounded leader with naive clients, for bounded + retry-after + full-jitter backoff (`b-jitter`), and for that plus a leader that serves its commit path first (`b-prio`). Two mechanisms do it, one per language:
   - **TS: the queue forms before admission.** The TS leader is CPU-bound, so at 1 × it falls behind on *reading*: requests wait in the kernel's socket buffers, where no bound and no refusal reach them (the leader refused almost nothing: `refused/s` 0). They pass the clients' 200 ms timeout, are retried (3-7 million retries a second), and the leader spends its core on attempts already abandoned. Pausing the sockets (`b-prio`) only moves the wait.
   - **Rust: refusals and retries swamp everyone.** The Rust leader parses fast and does refuse: 5.2 million refusals in the first second at 2 ×. But each refused write retries until its 2 s deadline (about eight times with a 10 ms base backoff), so the offered load becomes several times the capacity; commits fall to about zero, the leader idles at 0.1-0.3 of a core, and the clients spend their CPU on refusals and timers.
   Retry-after and jitter spread the retries in time; they do not bound how many there are.
   **Patient clients do not save it either** (`b-jitter-t2`: no timeout before 2 s, give up at 4 s): at 1 × the TS pipeline still delivered almost nothing (0.1-0.2 % of max) and Rust half (50 %), at 2 × TS nothing and Rust 3 %, with admitted p99 of 2-2.5 s (120-256 × steady), because nothing bounds the queue that forms in front of the leader.
3. **A client-side credit window works.** With each client holding at most `limit` writes outstanding (5 ms of the maximum; the rest wait in the client and are given up at their deadline), goodput at 2 × stays at 79-82 % of max (TS) and 88-89 % (Rust), admitted p99 stays at 0.8 × steady (12-14 ms TS, 8 ms Rust), refusals and retries almost vanish (a few hundred a second), nothing times out, and after the drop to 0.5 × the tail is back to steady in 0-6 s (F-19: 10 s). The cost moves to the client: writes past the window wait locally and are given up at the deadline (end-to-end p99 about 2 s at 1.5 × and 2 ×), which is the honest answer at twice capacity.

### What the numbers say: the reconnect storm

4. **Plain TCP: 10,000 subscribers are back in 1.5 s, with the tail untouched** (storm p99 1.2 × steady), whether clients redial every 20 ms or with jittered backoff. The new server started in 0.15-0.24 s holding each room's last 64 messages; clients caught up 8,700-9,800 missed messages from their cursors, with no shortfall.
5. **TLS: everyone is back in 5.6-7.1 s (under 10 s), but the tail is not.** 10,000 full TLS 1.3 handshakes take the new server's one thread about 0.8 of a core for 7 s; live deliveries to the clients already back wait behind them, so the storm p99 is 100-110 ms, 27-32 × steady (bar 2 ×). Session resumption (shared ticket keys, every reconnect resumed) cuts the server's CPU to 0.67 of a core and the time to 5.6 s, but the p99 stays high (156 ms, 45 ×): the handshakes come faster and still block the same thread. Jittered backoff (cap 3 s) did not spread the reconnects enough to matter.


## What this box cannot show

- **Three disks and three machines.** The three replicas share one disk and four cores with the clients (the clients' CPU is reported). Overload here is CPU and the one disk together.
- **Many groups and tenants.** One group; F-19's "other groups and tenants are unaffected" and the "1,000 groups failing over together" need the sim or many processes.
- **Real client populations.** Two client processes stand in for many clients; real clients have their own timeouts, libraries and networks.
- **A real restart.** The new server is a fresh process that starts holding the history; recovering it from a log on disk (E-34) would lengthen the restart.

## Verdict

**Bounded queues plus retry-after are not enough; admission control is a mechanism to price now, in two places.**

- **Writes.** At or above capacity, every combination without a client-side credit window collapsed to near-zero goodput and stayed there after the load fell, in TS and in Rust: in TS the queue forms in socket buffers before any bound can see it; in Rust the refusals work, but retries until a deadline multiply the offered load. A credit window (each client or link may hold at most `limit` writes outstanding, granted by the server; writes past it wait in the client, which fails them at their deadline) kept goodput at 79-82 % (TS) and 88-89 % (Rust) of max at 2 ×, admitted p99 at 0.8 × steady, and recovery within 6 s. So F-19's goodput bar is met only with credits (TS sits right on the 80 % line), and credits are part of the wire and the client API (L0, L3): a grant per link, a client-side queue with a deadline, retry only on an explicit refusal.
- **Reconnects.** Plain TCP resubscribes 10,000 clients in 1.5 s with the tail untouched, and cursors plus 64 messages of history per room left no shortfall, so server-side cursor tokens are not needed for this size. Over TLS everyone is back in 5.6-7.1 s (bar 10 s), but the handshakes block the server's one thread and the tail rises 27-45 × (bar 2 ×), even with session resumption. So the TLS storm needs admission too: a bound on handshakes per tick (accept fewer, let the rest wait in the backlog) or handshakes off the delivery thread (a TLS terminator, which E-27 also asks for).

Q-39: no; at 1-2 × the measured maximum (TS 1.53 M/s, Rust 2.62 M/s, E-02's pipeline with a client front), the unbounded queue, the bounded queue with refusals, bounded + retry-after + full-jitter backoff, and that plus a commit-path-first leader all collapsed to 0-50 % goodput and did not recover at 0.5 × (TS: the backlog sits in socket buffers before admission; Rust: retries until a 2 s deadline multiply the load), while a client-side credit window of 5 ms of capacity kept 79-82 % (TS) and 88-89 % (Rust) goodput at 2 × with admitted p99 0.8 × steady and recovery in 0-6 s; a 10,000-subscriber reconnect storm resubscribed in 1.5 s over TCP (p99 1.2 × steady) and 5.6-7.1 s over TLS (p99 27-45 × steady, resumption or not). Moves: every candidate prices admission control now as one mechanism in DEV-01 (credit windows on the client wire, plus a handshake budget at the edge), resolving X17's overload half; F-19 holds only with it (TS on the 80 % line, Rust 88 %); RUST, SRV, SIDE and EMB-V8 up a little on F-19 (more headroom, a cheaper refusal path); candidates with a TS TLS edge down on F-19's storm clause until TLS moves off the delivery thread (with E-27: a TLS terminator); no candidate needs server-side cursor tokens for resubscription at 10,000 (cursors plus a short history sufficed).
