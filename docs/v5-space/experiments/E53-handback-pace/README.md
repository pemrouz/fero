# E-53: how fast the hand-back evens out leaders, and what it costs the writes

**Question (Q-53).** V5-7 kept Raft's random timers and changed the hand-back's pace: from "at most ten leaderships a second per member" to "a cap on transfers in flight per member, busiest groups first". It claims (reasoned, not measured) that a member restart then evens out in seconds, not minutes, and that no rule is added. So: on real code, how long does leadership take to even out after one member restarts, and after a whole-project cold boot by random timers, under each pace? What does the hand-back cost the writes on the groups it moves? And does a cap need rules of its own?

**Threshold.** Two lines. (1) V5-7's own claim: after a restart, the hand-back takes seconds, not minutes, inside E-49's working range (up to about 10,000 resources per process); here read as "the hand-back ends within 30 s after `settle.ms`, so leaders are even within a minute of the member's return". (2) F-05 ([REQUIREMENTS.md](../../REQUIREMENTS.md)): "a planned handover pauses writes ≤ 10 ms (TS)". F-05 names no percentile, so the answer depends on how it is read, and this page gives three readings for the writes that meet their own group's transfer. **(a)** Their p99 minus the steady state's p99 before the hand-back. This was the first version's only reading. It was chosen so that the box's own tail (5.9-10.2 ms at p99 here, before any hand-back) is not charged to the hand-back; but it takes the tail off twice, since the caught writes' p99 holds the box's tail too. **(b)** Their p99 minus the steady state's p50: what such a write waits beyond a typical write. **(c)** Their longest wait, raw: E-13's reading, which timed the longest gap between answers around a transfer, a maximum. Section 8 gives all three; the verdict names the reading under which each cap passes.

**When it ran.** The pace runs ran from about 20:31 UTC on 2026-11-13 (the last smoke run's file time) to 00:33 on 11-14, and the first liveness check between them (it ended at 23:54). The host gained patches 13 and 14 part way through the pace runs: the first 11 lines of [results/handback.jsonl](results/handback.jsonl) (10 runs and the crashed one) were written before patch 13's counter existed, and patch 14 is dated 23:41. Neither fired in a measured window: patch 13's counter is 0 in every run that has it (measured), and patch 14 acts only while a member dials a dead peer, which no measured window holds (reasoned). The `load` target ran from 00:34 to 01:18 (lines 1-56 of [results/load.jsonl](results/load.jsonl), by its own clock, `load_t0`). The machine restarted at 01:19. Every finished run was kept (each is one whole line in `results/`; none was cut), and the missing load runs (lines 57-90) ran from 01:21 to 01:42 and the `settle` runs until 01:53, under the same lock, Node (22.22.0) and host; the guest kernel build changed from v445 to v470 ([results/versions.txt](results/versions.txt)). Section 3 compares the load runs from before and after: hand-back times and median latencies agree, but the latency tail was noisier after. The runs added after the skeptic's check (below) ran from 02:55 to 04:31 on 11-14, on kernel v470.

**Checked.** A skeptic's check found 15 problems; this page carries the fixes. The main ones. (1) Counting the cap at the giver also needs a rule: a group whose taker died goes quiet with its transfer still set, so Raft's own abort never fires and the slot stays taken (measured here with a new probe). So the cap adds a rule in both forms, not only at the taker. (2) Cap 8's pass of F-05 after a restart was not firm: the skeptic's rerun had one run of two 19 ms over the base. F-05's cells were run again, 10 runs each (5 at 100,000), with the writes that met a transfer marked and a like-for-like control (section 8), and cap 4 was added at 10,000. Cap 8 after a restart is near the bar (over it in 3 of 10 runs at 10,000), cap 4 was inside in 10 of 10, and after a cold boot even the first version's one pass (cap 8 at 1,000) did not hold up. (3) The design pushes the hand-back from each group's leader; this harness pulls it from the taker. The rule count is now given for both forms, and no record in SIMPLICITY counts the hand-back at all, so section 9 proposes one. (4) The host had one bug: a write held for a transfer did not wake a quiet group. It is fixed (patch 15); it was not hit in any measured run. The rest are smaller corrections, each marked where it applies.

## The answer, first

**A cap evens leaders out in seconds; ten a second takes minutes, just as E-49 computed.** After one member restarts, its groups (a third of all) are handed back to it. At ten a second that took 33 s at 1,000 resources per process, 5.6 minutes at 10,000 and (computed from the 60 s it ran) 56 minutes at 100,000. With a cap of 8, 32 or 128 transfers in flight it took 0.1-0.2 s, 0.5-1.3 s and 5-12 s. After a whole-project cold boot by random timers two thirds of all leaders are off preferred; ten a second took 24 s, 4.2 minutes and (computed) 41 minutes, a cap 0.1-0.2 s, 0.7-1.7 s and 7-15 s. 99% of groups were back a few tenths of a second before the last. In all 150 timed hand-backs (168 timed runs counting the 18 load controls with no hand-back) no transfer failed or had to be retried, and no election was started by a timer: every election was a transfer, one per moved group. So V5-7's claim holds on real code (measured): with cap 8, leaders are even about 31-32 s after a restarted member is back, up to 10,000 resources per process (`settle.ms`'s 30 s, plus 0.4-0.7 s to rebuild its groups and learn their leaders, plus the hand-back), and about 45 s after it is back at 100,000. Waiting the design's 30 s before the hand-back, not the harness's 10 s, did not change it (11.0 against 11.3 s after a restart at 100,000; 14.3 against 13.6 s after a cold boot).

**What the hand-back costs the writes grows with the cap.** While a capped hand-back runs, the members' one JavaScript thread is busy with transfers, so every write on them waits a little, not only the writes whose own group moves. With 300 loaded groups and cap 8, other writes were 2-13 ms slower at p99 than in a control window with no hand-back (measured; one cell, a cold boot at 100,000, is mostly the box's own noise). That lasts as long as the hand-back: 1.4-1.7 s at 10,000. At cap 32 a caught write's p99 cost is about 2-5 times cap 8's, at cap 128 3-9 times. Ten a second costs writes nothing measurable, but leaves the idle groups uneven for minutes. Of 6.45 million writes in the first runs none was lost and none timed out, and none of 4.38 million in the reruns either.

**F-05 (a handover pauses writes at most 10 ms) is near the bar at cap 8, not firmly inside it** (measured, 10 runs a cell after the skeptic's check; section 8). The answer depends on how F-05 is read. Read as the first version did (the caught writes' p99 minus the steady state's p99), cap 8 after a restart is +6 to +8 ms at the median, and over +10 in 1 of 10 runs at 1,000, 3 of 10 at 10,000 and 0 of 5 at 100,000. The box alone, in the same-shaped window with no transfer, went over +10 in only 1 of 80 windows, so the overs are mostly the hand-back's own cost. Cap 4 after a restart at 10,000 was inside in 10 of 10 runs (+3.8 ms at the median), with a hand-back of 2.2 s instead of 1.4 s. After a cold boot cap 8 is over it under every reading (+13 to +17 ms at the median, inside in at most 1 of 10 runs), and cap 4 at 10,000 halves that (+9.5, inside in 6 of 10). Read more strictly, against a typical write (p99 minus the steady state's p50) or by the longest wait (E-13's reading), no cap measured meets F-05 after a restart: a caught write waits 13 ms over a typical write at p99 at 10,000 with cap 8 (9 ms with cap 4), and its longest wait is 13-17 ms.

**Recommended default: a cap of 8, busiest first, or 4 where F-05 must hold** (a judgement on measured numbers). Cap 8 is the cheapest cap measured that evens out in about 1.5 s up to 10,000 (at most 2.2 s with load); its hand-back is already small next to `settle.ms`. A larger cap saves under a second up to 10,000 (4-6 s at 100,000) and costs caught writes 2-9 times more. Cap 4 is the cap that met F-05's first reading after a restart in every run at 10,000; it adds under a second there. Cap 4 was run only at 10,000. At 100,000 a full garbage collection stops a process for 110-261 ms (E-49), so no cap keeps a write that meets one inside 10 ms, and over a 10 s window with no hand-back the box was already +11.1 ms at p99 after a cold boot (measured). The caught writes, packed into the hand-back's first 0.3-0.4 s by busiest first, missed every full collection in the 10 reruns at 100,000 (their longest wait was 15-31 ms), so their +7 to +13 ms there is the hand-back's own cost (measured). Busiest first put all loaded groups back 0.02-0.10 s into the hand-back at the median, at most 0.24 s.

**V5-7's "no rule is added" does not hold: the cap adds a rule wherever it is counted** (measured; section 7). Counted at the taker (this harness), a transfer asked of a leader that then dies never ends, so the cap needs a timeout. Without it groups were never handed back in 20 of 20 runs; at cap 8, 5-8 of 8 slots stuck per member, and a member's whole hand-back stopped in 3 of 10 runs. Counted at the giver, Raft's own abort would free the slot, but it runs only while the group ticks, and a group whose taker died goes quiet with its transfer still set: 30-40 s after the kill, 1-30 such groups per run were still stuck, all quiet, in 22 of 22 runs. So the giver's form needs "a group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet" (none was stuck with it, 6 of 6 runs), or the same timeout. The design pushes the hand-back from each group's leader, while this harness pulls it from the taker; a cap per taker in the push form would also need a `busy` answer and a back-off (reasoned, not run). And no record counts the hand-back at all: section 9 proposes one, 4 rules (3 to 7), with this +1 inside it.

**How sure.** High for the times to even out and for "no failures, no timer elections" (measured on real Raft code, three runs each, narrow ranges). High that the cap needs a rule at the taker (measured on real code: groups never handed back in every run without it). Medium to high at the giver (the stuck, quiet transfers are measured in every run; a cap per giver was not built, so that its slots then stay taken is reasoned from that state and the code). Medium for the costs to writes and for F-05 (one box with 4 shared vCPUs, the client on it too; p99 over a few hundred caught writes a run, packed into the first 0.3-0.4 s of the hand-back by busiest first, so run-to-run spread is wide; 10 runs a cell). Medium for the pick of 8 or 4 (cap 4 was run only at 10,000). Low to medium for how three real machines would do, and for the push form (reasoned: a transfer 1-2 ms longer, the busy-thread cost spread over three machines, and the same transfer cost whoever starts it).

## Words used here

- **Group**: one Raft group. Here each resource is one group (its home group, V5-2), as in E-49.
- **Member**: one process holding a copy of every group. Here 3 processes on one box, so 3 copies.
- **Preferred member** (preferred leader): the member that should lead a group when all are up (DESIGN-V5 4.2: the one the ring ranks first; here member g % 3 + 1, as in E-26 and E-49). **Off preferred**: a group led by another member.
- **Transfer** (a planned handover): Raft's leadership transfer. The leader brings the target up to its log end, then sends it TimeoutNow; the target stands for election at once and wins (DESIGN-V5 4.2; E-13).
- **Taker** and **giver**: in a transfer, the member that becomes leader and the one that stops.
- **Hand-back**: once a preferred member has been back and caught up for `settle.ms` (30 s), its groups' leadership is handed back to it by transfers.
- **Push** and **pull**: who starts each transfer of a hand-back. Push: each group's leader (the giver) decides and starts it, as DESIGN-V5 4.2 writes ("the leader hands leadership to it"). Pull: the taker asks for it, as this harness does.
- **Pace**: how fast the hand-back goes. **Ten a second**: at most ten transfers begun a second per member (DESIGN-V5 4.2 before V5-7). **Cap K**: at most K transfers in flight per member; a new one begins as soon as one ends (V5-7). A **slot** is one of the K places; a transfer holds one until it ends. A cap **per taker** counts the transfers into a member; a cap **per giver** counts the transfers out of it.
- **Busiest first**: the groups with the most recent writes go first (V5-7's order). **Random**: no order.
- **Even**: every group led by its preferred member. **Within 1%**: at most 1% of all groups still off preferred.
- **Quiet group**: an idle group that stops its heartbeats (E-11).
- **Timer election**: an election begun because a timer ran out, not because of a transfer.
- **Caught write**: a write planned between 50 ms before its own group's transfer began and 200 ms after it ended. **Other write**: a write during the hand-back on a group not in a transfer then.
- **Met a transfer** (added after the skeptic's check): a write that was answered `moved` (it went to the old leader) or waited at its leader for the transfer to end. Most caught writes did neither: they ran before or after the move.
- **Base**: the steady state before the hand-back: the median, over every run of the same scenario and R, of the writes' latency in the 8 s before it began.
- **Control**: a window with no hand-back. The first runs' control is a whole 10 s window at the same point (pace none). The **like-for-like control** (added after the skeptic's check) takes the same loaded groups' caught windows from the same run and shifts them back into the steady state before the hand-back: the same writes, the same packing in time, and no transfer.
- **Little's law**: in a steady flow, things finished per second = things in flight / time each takes.

## Method

One harness, in this folder, on the box E-11, E-26 and E-49 used (4 shared vCPUs, 15 GB, Node 22.22.0; [results/versions.txt](results/versions.txt)). Nothing was rebuilt: it is E-49's three-process setup on E-26's host, with E-49's own patches, plus only what a hand-back needs.

**The host** ([host-patch.mjs](host-patch.mjs)). E-26's host ([../E26-dormant-groups/host.ts](../E26-dormant-groups/host.ts): E-11's TypeScript host on E-10's port of etcd raft, quiet groups, link pings every 500 ms, one journal with one fdatasync per round of the loop) is patched into a copy at `$SP/e53/host/host.ts`; E-26's file is not changed. Patches 1-4 are E-49's ([boot.mjs](../E49-per-resource-cost/boot.mjs)), copied word for word: Raft's random timers with DESIGN-V5's timing (100 ms ticks, `election.ms` 2 s plus up to 500 ms), no campaign at start, a `leaders` command. Patches 5-18 are new, each marked in the copy (15-18 after the skeptic's check):

| patch | what | why the hand-back needs it |
|---|---|---|

| 7 | counters on Raft's own steps: elections begun by a timer (pre-candidacies), candidacies, transfer campaigns, leaderships won | elections caused |
| 8 | a client port: write(g, seq) in; ack on commit, or `moved` with the leader the member knows. A write that reaches a leader in the middle of a transfer waits for it ([host-patch.mjs:191-206](host-patch.mjs)) | the cost to writes; DESIGN-V5 4.2: "writes that arrive meanwhile wait, or get moved with the target"; V5-9: a client writes to its slot's leader. The port itself drops a proposal during a transfer (code: [raft.ts:773](../E10-raft-port/port/src/raft.ts)), and E-13 found that dropping costs the client its timeout |

**Pull, not push.** In this harness each member asks for its own groups back (pull), so the cap counts the transfers a member has **asked for** and not yet won: a cap per taker. The design pushes: DESIGN-V5 4.2 has each group's leader hand leadership to the preferred member ([DESIGN-V5.md:432](../../../DESIGN-V5.md)), and planner-hints@per-resource's rule 5 says "no leader hands leadership back to it while it drains" (docs: [judging/simplicity.json](../../judging/simplicity.json)). A leader that decides counts most easily what it gives: a cap per giver, which lets a restarted member take up to K from each live peer at once, 2K on three machines (reasoned). V5-7's "a cap on transfers in flight per member" replaces DESIGN-V5's "a member takes back at most ten leaderships a second", which counts at the taker, so V5-7's cap most likely means per taker (reasoned from the wording). In the push form a cap per taker needs the taker to grant or refuse each leader's transfer, for example a `busy` answer and a back-off, as snapshot pacing has ([DESIGN-V5.md:433](../../../DESIGN-V5.md)); that adds rules and was not run. The measured times should carry over to the push form: one transfer costs the same whoever starts it (reasoned). The rules differ, and section 9 counts them for each form.

**The runs** ([handback.mjs](handback.mjs)). Three processes, R groups each, R = 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000.
- **Restart.** The three boot with every group led by its preferred member (E-26's start campaign, used only to reach a balanced start fast). Then `kill -9` of member 2. Its two peers see the link die and elect for its groups (timed: the failover). Member 2 starts again from its journal; it is back when every group it holds knows its leader (timed). After `settle` (below), the hand-back gives back about R/3 groups, all to member 2.
- **Cold boot.** The three boot by Raft's random timers (the base's boot, E-49 section 6), so about two thirds of the leaders land off preferred. After `settle`, every member takes back its own, about 2R/3 transfers in all, each member at once a taker and a giver.
- **Paces.** Ten a second, cap 8, cap 32, cap 128; random order. Ten a second at 100,000 would run 41-56 minutes, so it stops after 60 s and the end is computed from the rate it held (the 1,000 and 10,000 runs ran to the end).
- **Load** ([results/load.jsonl](results/load.jsonl)). [client.mjs](client.mjs), a fourth process, writes 100 B to 300 random groups, 10 writes a second each (3,000 a second), open loop: each write leaves at its planned time, whatever came back. It sends each write to the member it thinks leads the group and follows `moved` answers. Latency is the committed answer's arrival minus the planned time, so it carries any wait, move or resend. The load starts when `settle` starts and stops 6 s after the hand-back ends. Configurations at R = 1,000 and 10,000: no hand-back (the control); cap 32 random; cap 8, 32 and 128 busiest first; ten a second busiest first (stopped after 30 s: by then the loaded groups have long moved). At 100,000: the control, cap 8 and cap 32 busiest first.
- **A giver dies** (the liveness check, [results/liveness.jsonl](results/liveness.jsonl); not a timing, so it ran under `$SP/bin/heavy`). A cold boot at R = 1,000 and 10,000, cap 32; member 3 is killed 50 ms or 200 ms into the hand-back. Do members 1 and 2 still take back every group they are preferred for, with the cap's 3 s timeout and without it? Five runs each.
- **Added after the skeptic's check.** (i) **F-05 again** ([results/recheck.jsonl](results/recheck.jsonl), timed): restart and cold boot, busiest first, the same 300 loaded groups' load, cap 8 at R = 1,000 and 10,000 (10 runs each) and at 100,000 (5 runs, with `settle` 20 s so the like-for-like control fits in the steady state before the hand-back), and cap 4 at 10,000 (10 runs). The runs of each scenario are interleaved (run 1 of every cell, then run 2, and so on) inside one timing hold per scenario. The writes that met a transfer are marked (patch 16). (ii) **Cap 8 when a giver dies** ([results/liveness.jsonl](results/liveness.jsonl), heavy lock): as the liveness check, with and without the timeout, 5 runs without it at each R and 3 with it. (iii) **The givers' side** ([results/xfer.jsonl](results/xfer.jsonl) and the cap-8 liveness lines, heavy lock): in the same kill, member 3 was also a taker, so members 1 and 2 hold transfers to it. About 30 s after the kill (40 s where the hand-back never ended) each survivor counts the groups it leads with a transfer still set and how many are quiet, then one write reaches each such group and they are counted again 3 s later (patch 17). Cap 32 as the first check, 3 runs at each R, as designed and with patch 18.
- **`settle`.** The harness waits 10 s, not the design's 30 s, before the hand-back; the time to even out after a restart is then the design's 30 s plus the hand-back measured here. One set at 100,000 used 30 s, cap 8, to check that the wait does not change the hand-back ([results/settle.jsonl](results/settle.jsonl)).

**What is measured, per run.** From each member's own record of every transfer (when it asked, when it won), on one clock: the time from the hand-back's start until every group is on its preferred member, and until 99% are; each transfer's time; failed transfers (retried) and transfers skipped because the member did not yet know the leader. Elections: Raft's own steps counted over the hand-back (timer elections = candidacies minus transfer campaigns). CPU per process from `/proc` in 250 ms slices: CPU-seconds over the hand-back, and the most in any one second. With load: commit latency of the writes before, during and after the hand-back, of the caught writes, and of the other writes during it.

**Timing rules.** Every timed run held `$SP/bin/timing`, so no other timed run or heavy job overlapped it. The first run of the paces held it once per pace (over its R values); from the second run on, once per run and scenario (all paces, 10-25 minutes), because other sessions' heavy jobs slipped in between short holds and the matrix waited for each. Load averages are in each raw line. Three runs of everything (ten of section 8's cells); tables show the median and the range. The runs added after the skeptic's check held the lock once per scenario (section 8) or ran under the heavy lock (section 7). The restart scenario's balanced start uses E-26's start campaign; at 100,000 with DESIGN-V5's 100 ms ticks it collided in some runs (an 83 s boot that left one member with almost none of its leaderships, as E-49 saw once with 500 ms ticks), so the harness then balances the leaders first with a cap-128 hand-back before the kill (setup, not measured; [results/tables.md](results/tables.md) counts them). The first such run crashed a host with a snapshot message and is kept in [results/handback.jsonl](results/handback.jsonl) as failed; patch 13 fixed it and the run was done again.

## Results

All tables are generated by [summarize.py](summarize.py) into [results/tables.md](results/tables.md); the raw lines are [results/handback.jsonl](results/handback.jsonl), [results/load.jsonl](results/load.jsonl), [results/settle.jsonl](results/settle.jsonl) and [results/liveness.jsonl](results/liveness.jsonl), and from the skeptic's check [results/recheck.jsonl](results/recheck.jsonl) and [results/xfer.jsonl](results/xfer.jsonl). Medians of three runs (ten in section 8), range in brackets. Measured here unless marked. Sections 1-6 are the first runs; section 7 gained cap 8 and the givers' side, and sections 8 and 9 are new.

### 1. Time to even out, no load

The hand-back alone, from its start (after `settle`) until every group is led by its preferred member. The time after a member's return is the design's `settle.ms` (30 s) plus this.

| R per process | groups to hand back | ten a second | cap 8 | cap 32 | cap 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **restart**: 1,000 | 333 | 33.2 s | 0.2 s | 0.1 s | 0.1 s |
| 10,000 | 3,333 | **333 s (5.6 min)** | 1.3 s | 0.7 s | 0.5 s |
| 100,000 | 33,333 | **3,336 s (56 min)**, computed | 11.3 s (10.9-12.1) | 6.7 s (6.2-7.2) | 5.0 s (4.3-5.6) |
| **cold boot**: 1,000 | about 660 | 24.1 s (23.8-24.8) | 0.2 s | 0.2 s | 0.1 s |
| 10,000 | about 6,650 | **251 s (4.2 min)** (242-254) | 1.5 s (1.4-1.7) | 0.9 s | 0.7 s (0.6-0.8) |
| 100,000 | about 66,700 | **2,475 s (41 min)**, computed | 13.6 s (12.9-14.5) | 9.5 s (8.9-9.7) | 7.6 s (7.1-8.7) |

- **Ten a second is exactly as slow as E-49 computed.** After a restart the hand-back took 33.2 s at 1,000 and 333.3 s at 10,000 (R/30 s, in every run), and at 100,000 it held ten a second for the 60 s it ran (computed end: 56 minutes). After a cold boot each member takes back its own two ninths of the groups at once, so it took 24 s, 4.2 minutes and (computed) 41 minutes.
- **A cap evens out in seconds.** Up to 10,000 resources per process the hand-back is over in 0.1-1.7 s at any cap; at 100,000 in 4.3-14.5 s. 99% of groups are back 0.0-0.4 s before the last ([results/tables.md](results/tables.md)).
- **Nothing failed.** In all 72 timed runs without load no transfer failed or was retried, no transfer was skipped for want of a known leader, and no election began by a timer during a hand-back. Every term rise was a transfer: terms rose by exactly one per moved group on each member, and Raft's transfer campaigns equalled the groups moved, in all 150 timed hand-backs with or without load (measured; 168 timed runs counting the 18 load controls with no hand-back).
- **The CPU.** A cap packs the work into a short time: the taker's busiest second was 0.2-0.9 of a core up to 10,000, and 0.9-1.4 cores at 100,000 after a restart (1.5-1.7 cores per process after a cold boot at 100,000, where each member is taker and giver). More than one core is possible because V8's collector runs on helper threads; `/proc` counts them. Ten a second spreads it: the busiest second was at most 0.33 of a core up to 10,000 (1.1-1.6 cores at 100,000, in one second of the 60 s: a collector pause, reasoned). A slow hand-back is not cheaper in all: after a restart at 10,000 the three processes spent 18.5 CPU-seconds over the ten-a-second hand-back (5.5 ms per transfer, most of it their idle cost over 333 s) against 1.5-2.5 CPU-seconds with a cap (0.4-0.8 ms per transfer).

### 2. What sets a cap's speed

| R | pace | restart: transfers a second | restart: one transfer, ms p50 / p99 | cold boot: transfers a second | cold boot: one transfer, ms p50 / p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | ten a second | 10 | 3.2 / 6.8 | 26 (three takers) | 2.8 / 7.0 |
| | cap 8 | 2,523 | 2.5 / 10.5 | 4,425 | 4.0 / 17.3 |
| | cap 32 | 4,694 | 5.5 / 18.9 | 7,533 | 10.0 / 36.4 |
| | cap 128 | 6,953 | 16.1 / 37.9 | 9,153 | 31.8 / 107 |
| 100,000 | cap 8 | 2,938 | 2.0 / 11.8 | 4,921 | 2.9 / 17.5 |
| | cap 32 | 5,011 | 4.5 / 21.3 | 7,039 | 10.6 / 32.7 |
| | cap 128 | 6,694 | 17.7 / 38.4 | 8,792 | 35.2 / 165 |

- **Little's law holds**: transfers a second ≈ the cap (times the number of takers) / one transfer's time. For example 32 / 5.5 ms ≈ 5,800 a second against 4,694 measured (the mean transfer is longer than the median). A transfer alone takes 2-4 ms here: two fsync rounds and a few loopback hops. (E-13, on etcd: a transfer added about 4 ms to a quiet 5.5 ms gap between answers, 9.8 ms in all at the median, 24-31 ms at worst.)
- **More in flight mostly makes each transfer wait longer.** From cap 8 to 128 one transfer's time grows 6-12 times while the rate grows only 1.8-2.8 times: the members' one JavaScript thread becomes the bottleneck. This box tops out at about 7,000 transfers a second into one member and about 9,000 a second in all (four shared vCPUs, three members).
- **For time alone, cap 32 is the knee.** It cuts cap 8's time by 30-50% at 10,000 and 100,000; cap 128 saves only another 0.2 s (10,000) or 1.7-1.9 s (100,000), and makes each transfer 3-4 times longer (p99 up to 165 ms after a cold boot at 100,000). The slowest single transfers, 180-400 ms at 100,000 at every cap, match the collector's full pauses there (E-49: 110-261 ms; reasoned). Section 4 shows why time alone is not the right way to pick.

- **Ten a second costs writes nothing you can measure.** Caught writes are 0-5 ms slower at p99, other writes stay within the control's noise. (One run, after the machine restart, had a 0.6 s stall of the whole box in seconds 14-15 of a ten-a-second hand-back at 1,000, where transfers run at ten a second and the collector pauses 5-11 ms: a box stall, not the hand-back; reasoned.)
- **A cap slows every write on the members while it runs, not only the caught ones.** The members' one JavaScript thread is busy with transfers, so all writes queue behind them. The cost grows with the cap: other writes' p99 rises by 2-15 ms over the base with cap 8 (2-13 ms over the control's), by 10-57 ms with cap 32 (149 ms after a cold boot at 100,000, where the collector adds its own pauses) and by 14-84 ms with cap 128. One cell is mostly the box's own noise: after a cold boot at 100,000 the control's own window is already +11.1 ms at p99 (one of its three runs was -2.4, two were +11 to +12), so cap 8's +13.1 there is only +2.0 over it (-4.5 to +115 by run). After a restart at 100,000, cap 8's +12.8 against the control's +1.2 is a real cost (measured). At p50 the cost stays small: 1-5 ms for caught writes at cap 8.
- **Caught writes pay a little more** than other writes at the same cap. The first version said this was because they also wait for their own transfer, and the first write after a move is answered `moved` and sent again. The rerun that marked those writes (section 8) shows that explains little: the writes that met a transfer had about the same p99 as the caught writes that met none (cap 8 after a restart: +5.3 to +6.5 against +6.0 to +7.4 ms over the base p99, medians by R; measured). Most caught writes met no transfer: they came just after their group moved to the taker, whose one thread was running the next transfers (reasoned from when they ran). After a cold boot it is the other way: the met writes carry more of the tail (section 8).
- **No write was lost.** Over all first runs 6.45 million writes were answered (and 4.38 million in section 8's reruns); none was left unanswered and none timed out. Per configuration, over its three runs, 2-74 writes reached their leader during a transfer and waited for it, and about one write per moved loaded group got a `moved` answer (about 100 a run after a restart, 200 after a cold boot), as expected.
- **Busiest first does what V5-7 wants.** With any cap, all loaded groups were back on their preferred member 0.02-0.10 s into the hand-back at the median, at most 0.24 s (one cold boot at 100,000, cap 32); with random order, 0.8-1.1 s at 10,000 (at 1,000 the whole hand-back is 0.1 s). With ten a second, busiest first brought the loaded groups back in 7-11 s, while the idle rest took minutes. The order barely changes a caught write's cost (cap 32: +17.5 against +17.7 ms after a restart at 10,000; +35 against +43 after a cold boot).
- **Runs before and after the machine restart** ([results/tables.md](results/tables.md), "Runs before and after"). Of the 30 load configurations, 26 have two runs from before and one from after, 4 the other way round. Hand-back times agree: the same or up to 40% longer after (cap 8: 14.0 against 15.5 s after a restart at 100,000). The steady state's p50 agrees (1.7-2.2 ms before, 1.7-2.0 after). Its p99 was higher after the restart in 28 of 30 configurations, mostly by 0.1-10 ms, and five single windows held one stall of 46-330 ms; caught writes' p99 was higher too in most. So the box was noisier at the tail after the restart (cause not found; the guest kernel build changed). The medians above lean on the earlier runs, the upper ends of the ranges often come from the later one, and the cost tables use a base pooled over all three runs.

### 4. Which cap

| pace | even after a restart: 10,000 / 100,000 | even after a cold boot: 10,000 / 100,000 | caught writes, extra p99 (reading a): restart / cold boot | other writes, extra p99 over the control's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ten a second | 5.6 min / 56 min | 4.2 min / 41 min | -0.4 to 2.0 / 0.4 to 4.9 ms | 0 to 1.5 ms |
| cap 4 (10,000 only; section 8) | 2.2 s with load / not run | 2.3 s with load / not run | 3.8 / 9.5 ms (10 runs) | not compared |
| **cap 8** | **1.3 s / 11.3 s** | **1.5 s / 13.6 s** | **6.1 to 7.6 / 13.0 to 16.6 ms (10 runs; 5 at 100,000)** | **2 to 13 ms** |
| cap 32 | 0.7 s / 6.7 s | 0.9 s / 9.5 s | 11.8 to 18.0 / 30 to 65 ms | 10 to 56 ms (138 once) |
| cap 128 | 0.5 s / 5.0 s | 0.7 s / 7.6 s | 32 to 69 / 54 to 92 ms | 14 to 82 ms |

The caught-write column is the median over runs for each R; cap 8's and cap 4's come from section 8's reruns, the others from the first runs (three a cell).

- **Cap 8 is the one to make the default, or cap 4 where F-05 must hold** (measured; the pick is a judgement on the measured numbers). Cap 8 already evens leaders out in 1.3-1.7 s up to 10,000 resources per process, and in 11-17 s at 100,000. That is small next to `settle.ms` (30 s), which is now most of the wait (section 5). Of the caps first measured it costs writes the least: after a restart a caught write is +6 to +8 ms at p99 at the median, which is near F-05's 10 ms, and over it in 1 to 3 runs of 10 (section 8); other writes are 2-13 ms slower than the control's, for 0.2-1.4 s up to 10,000 (14 s at 100,000). Cap 4 after a restart at 10,000 kept every one of 10 runs inside reading (a), at 2.2 s for the hand-back instead of 1.4 s (measured).
- **A larger cap buys little and costs a lot.** Cap 32 saves 0.6 s at 10,000 and 4-5 s at 100,000, and makes caught writes about 2-5 times slower at p99 than cap 8 (against cap 8's ten-run medians; 2-7 times against its three-run ones). Cap 128 saves another 0.2-1.9 s, and its caught writes are 3-9 times slower at p99 than cap 8's (5-26 times against the three-run ones).
- **Cap 8 is not inside F-05 after a cold boot** at any R (+13 to +17 ms at p99 at the median, inside in at most 1 of 10 runs; section 8): there every member takes back its own at once, so 24 transfers run on one box. A cold boot is rare (V5-7), and the cost lasts 1.6-2.2 s at 10,000. Cap 4 at 10,000 halves it (+9.5 ms at the median, inside in 6 of 10 runs), for a hand-back of 2.3 s instead of 1.6 s (measured; the first version reasoned this, not run). At 100,000 cap 4 was not run; there a full collection (110-261 ms, E-49) breaks F-05 for any write it lands on, whatever the cap.
- **Where the cap is counted changes the load, not whether a rule is needed.** Counted at the taker (as here), cap 8 means at most 8 transfers into a member, and it needs the timeout rule (section 7). Counted at the giver it needs a rule too: a group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet, or a timeout on the slot (section 7, measured; the first version said this form needed none, which was wrong). A cap of K per giver also lets a restarted member take up to K from each live peer at once: 2K on three machines, and more on more machines (reasoned). So a cap per giver that matches the load measured here would be about 4 (reasoned), with its rule.

- **A cap counted at the taker needs a timeout** (measured). Until the failover ends, a member keeps filling its free slots with groups still led by the dead giver. It wins some of those groups' elections, and its slot then frees; the groups the other survivor wins stay in their slots for ever, since nothing tells the taker. How many stick grows with the cap: about 20 of 32 slots per member at cap 32, and 5-8 of 8 at cap 8 (the first version's "about 20 slots stuck" was cap 32's count, not a fixed number). At cap 8 a member's whole hand-back stopped, all 8 slots stuck, in 3 of 10 runs, leaving 86, 1,739 and 1,659 of its groups never handed back; in the other runs 5-7 per member were never handed back, so some groups were never handed back in 10 of 10 runs. (The skeptic's own five runs at cap 8 found the same shape: 4-8 of 8 stuck, and both members stopped in 1 of 5.) With the 3 s timeout every group came back in about 5.5 s at both caps: the failover's 2.5 s plus one timeout.

**The givers' side** ([results/xfer.jsonl](results/xfer.jsonl), and the cap-8 lines of liveness.jsonl): the groups members 1 and 2 lead with a transfer to the dead member 3 still set (Raft's `leadTransferee`), about 30 s after the kill (40 s where the hand-back never ended), summed over both.

| 32 | 10,000 | as designed | 3 | 22, 30, 8 | all | all |
| 8 | 1,000 | as designed | 8 | 5, 1, 8, 2, 8, 5, 8, 5 | all | all |
| 8 | 10,000 | as designed | 8 | 4, 5, 2, 4, 8, 5, 6, 4 | all | all |
| 32 | 1,000 | no quiet in a transfer (patch 18) | 3 | 0, 0, 0 | - | - |
| 32 | 10,000 | no quiet in a transfer (patch 18) | 3 | 0, 0, 0 | - | - |

- **A cap counted at the giver needs a rule too** (the skeptic's finding F1, measured here). A giver's slot would be freed by Raft's own abort, but that abort runs only inside the leader's heartbeat tick (code: [raft.ts:350](../E10-raft-port/port/src/raft.ts)), and the host never ticks a quiet group (code: [host.ts:246](../E26-dormant-groups/host.ts), before the Tick at :259). DESIGN-V5 4.2's quiet rule ("whose live voters have all reached its commit offset", [DESIGN-V5.md:408](../../../DESIGN-V5.md)) has no exception for a transfer in flight, and a dead taker is not a live voter. So the group goes quiet two ticks after its taker dies, long before the abort's 20 ticks. Measured: in 22 of 22 runs, 30-40 s after the kill, the survivors still led 1-30 groups with the transfer set (3-17 per member at cap 32, 0-6 at cap 8), every one of them quiet. One write does not clear them: it wakes the group for two ticks, and then the group goes quiet again (measured: all still set 3 s later; code: host.ts:247-256). A steady stream of writes would keep the group awake until the abort, about 2 s, with every write held meanwhile (reasoned from the same code). Under a cap counted at the giver these transfers are the giver's own slots, so they stay taken until the taker comes back, a stream of writes wakes the group, or healing removes the dead voter after `evict.after` (Raft then aborts the transfer, [raft.ts:691](../E10-raft-port/port/src/raft.ts)). A giver holding as many such transfers as its cap stops giving (reasoned from the measured counts: 13-17 per member at cap 32 in some runs). When the taker does come back, a leader still holding the transfer can send it TimeoutNow on its next append answer ([raft.ts:856](../E10-raft-port/port/src/raft.ts)), so leadership can move to a member that has not yet been back for `settle.ms` (reasoned, not run). With patch 18, "a group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet", none was left in 6 of 6 runs: Raft's own abort fired. So the giver's form needs that rule, or a timeout of its own on the slot: +1 either way.
- **At any pace, the group itself is held too.** Raft drops every proposal while its transfer is set ([raft.ts:773](../E10-raft-port/port/src/raft.ts)); this host holds the write instead (patch 8). So a group whose taker died takes no writes until a stream of writes keeps it awake for `election.ms` (reasoned). Patch 18's rule ends that in `election.ms` whatever the pace. Before patch 15 a held write did not even wake the group (the skeptic's F13; no earlier measured run met this case, since the liveness check has no load).
- **A transfer that keeps failing must not keep blocking writes** (a property the first version missed, the skeptic's F14; reasoned, not run). Each attempt stops its group's writes until it is won or Raft aborts it after `election.ms`. With the timeout-and-retry rule, a taker that can never win would cost its group about 2 s of writes in every 3 s cycle, far past F-05's 10 ms. One such case in the pull form: a one-way link loss, where the taker's asks reach the leader but the leader's messages never reach the taker. In the push form the leader judges "caught up" from its own record of the taker's log, which a one-way loss stops, so that case starts no transfer there (reasoned). A retry therefore needs a back-off or a give-up: likely one more rule (reasoned; counted in section 9's high).
- Every pace needs a failed transfer tried again; that is the level-triggered rule DESIGN-V5 already has (hand back while the preferred member is live and caught up; docs).

### 9. The rules a cap needs, and a record for the hand-back

**No record counts the hand-back today** (docs: [judging/simplicity.json](../../judging/simplicity.json)). healing-self@per-resource's notes say the preferred-leader hand-back after `settle.ms` is "not in E-16's world and not counted"; planner-hints@per-resource (its rules 4 and 5), raft-leader-transfer and the slow-leader records only mention it. So V5-7's "no rule is added" and this page's first "+1" both sat on a mechanism with no count. The skeptic's F6 asks for one; here it is, with E-53's findings inside it.

**The rules each form of the cap adds** (section 7):

| form | who starts a transfer | where the cap counts | what frees a slot when a transfer cannot finish | rules added | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pull, cap per taker (this harness) | the taker asks | the taker | nothing in Raft: the taker is never told | +1: a transfer not won within `election.ms` plus jitter frees its slot and is tried again | measured: without it, groups never handed back in 20 of 20 runs (cap 32 and cap 8, one giver's crash) |
| push, cap per giver | the leader | the leader | Raft's abort, but only while the group ticks, and a group whose taker died goes quiet first | +1: a group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet (or the leader's own timeout on the slot) | measured: transfers still set, all quiet, 30-40 s after the kill in 22 of 22 runs; none with the rule, 6 of 6 |
| push, cap per taker (V5-7's likely meaning) | the leader, once the taker grants | the taker | both of the above | about +3 to +4: the taker answers `busy` past its cap, a leader that hears `busy` backs off, a grant not used within `election.ms` frees itself, and the giver's rule above | reasoned, not run |

**A proposed record, `handback@per-resource`** (count 4, low 3, high 7; basis: two rules measured in DEV-10's sense on real code, not model-checked, two from the docs, three reasoned):

1. Each group's leader hands leadership to the group's preferred member once that member has been live and caught up for `settle.ms` (DESIGN-V5 4.2; docs).
2. At most K hand-back transfers are in flight per member, busiest groups first (V5-7; K is a setting and the order a policy; it replaces "ten a second", which was a rule of the same kind; docs).
3. A hand-back transfer not finished within `election.ms` plus jitter frees its slot, and the group is tried again later (E-53: measured, needed in the pull form; dropping it leaves groups never handed back after one crash).
4. A group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet (E-53: measured, needed in the push form with a cap per giver; it also ends a dead taker's transfer in `election.ms` at any pace).

Low 3 merges rules 3 and 4 into one timer at whichever side counts the cap (the group's writes then stay held until a stream of writes wakes it). High 7 adds: (5) a transfer that failed is tried again only after a back-off, and given up until the taker's link or caught-up state changes (F14; reasoned); and, for a cap per taker in the push form, (6) the taker answers `busy` past its cap and (7) a leader that hears `busy` backs off, as snapshot pacing does (F5; reasoned). Against this record V5-7's change adds +1 (rule 3 or rule 4, by form; measured), or about +3 to +4 if its cap per member means per taker in the push form (reasoned).

## What this harness leaves out

- **Three machines on one box, over loopback.** A transfer here costs two fsync rounds and a few loopback hops. Across machines each hop adds a round trip (about 0.5-1 ms in one region, reasoned), so one transfer takes about 1-2 ms longer, and a cap moves fewer a second, by Little's law (reasoned). The three processes and the client share 4 vCPUs, so the members' CPU competes in a way three machines would not: on three machines the busy thread's cost to other writes (section 3) should be smaller (reasoned).
- **One group per resource, no rows.** Every group is a resource's home group with an empty fold. A resource with G groups owes G times the transfers (E-49 section 10). A transfer does not move rows: every full replica is warm (DESIGN-V5 4.2), so its time does not grow with a resource's size. But a new leader answers nothing until its fold reaches its log end; that wait is not here (the fold is empty).
- **Pull, not push.** The taker asks for each transfer; the design has each group's leader start it (Method, "Pull, not push"). The times should carry over, since a transfer costs the same whoever starts it (reasoned); the rules differ (section 9). A cap per giver was not timed (its rule was probed, section 7), and a cap per taker in the push form, which needs the taker to answer `busy`, was not built.
- **The member's own view.** The taker knows the leader from its own replica and asks it directly. The design's caught-up signal before `settle.ms` and the planner's `lead()` are not built; `settle` is a fixed wait.
- **Failure detection by the socket.** A killed process's links close at once on loopback, so the survivors start their election clocks at once. The design detects by missed link pings (`election.ms`), so a real failover starts up to `ping.ms` later (reasoned). A machine that vanishes without closing its sockets is not run.
- **The link-up wake.** When a link comes back, each leader wakes every group it leads, not only those whose follower is behind (DESIGN-V5's rule needs a log-end report per link, not built). This costs the restart a heartbeat round on every group; it ends before the hand-back starts.
- **Caps.** Timed: 8, 32 and 128 everywhere, and cap 4 only at R = 10,000 with load (section 8). Caps below 4 were not run.
- **A taker that can never win** (a one-way link loss, say) was not run: section 7's property that a failing transfer must not keep blocking writes is reasoned only.
- **Writes caught mid-transfer wait.** The host holds a write that reaches its leader during a transfer, as DESIGN-V5 4.2 allows ("wait, or get moved with the target"); since patch 15 such a write also wakes its group (anyone reusing the host from before the skeptic's check should take that fix). The simplicity record's `raft-leader-transfer` rule 2 refuses it with the target's name instead (docs: [judging/simplicity.json](../../judging/simplicity.json)). Few writes met that case (section 3: at most 74 per configuration over its three runs), so the choice moves little here (reasoned).
- **One load shape.** 300 groups at 10 writes a second each, 100 B, one client. A hotter group, or many more loaded groups, was not run.
- **The `settle` wait** is 10 s here, 30 s in the design (checked at 100,000 with cap 8, section 6; not with load).
- **Garbage collection** runs on V8's own timing; a hand-back or a write that lands on a major collection waits for it (E-49: 19-44 ms at 10,000 groups, 110-261 ms at 100,000).
- **Dormant groups** (E-26) were not run: a dormant group has no leader to hand back until it wakes (E-49, reasoned).

## Against the threshold

- **(1) V5-7's claim: the hand-back ends within 30 s after `settle.ms`.** Met by every cap at every R: at most 18.1 s (a cold boot at 100,000 with load, in the reruns), and at most 2.2 s at cap 8 up to 10,000 (2.6 s at cap 4) (measured). So with cap 8 leaders are even about 31-32 s after a restarted member is back up to 10,000 (33-35 s after the crash), and about 45 s after it is back at 100,000 (54 s after the crash) (measured parts, summed; section 5). Ten a second misses it after a restart at every R: 33 s at 1,000 (just over), 5.6 minutes at 10,000 and 56 minutes at 100,000 (computed). After a cold boot it meets it only at 1,000 (24.1 s). V5-7's "seconds, not minutes" holds (measured).
- **(2) F-05: a planned handover pauses writes at most 10 ms.** It depends on the reading (section 8). **(a) The caught writes' p99 minus the steady state's p99** (the first version's reading): after a restart, met by ten a second (at most +2 ms, first runs); near the bar at cap 8 (+6.1 to +7.6 ms at the median; inside in 9 of 10 runs at 1,000, 7 of 10 at 10,000 and 5 of 5 at 100,000); met by cap 4 at 10,000 in 10 of 10 runs (+3.8); missed by cap 32 (+11.8 to +18.0) and cap 128 (+32 to +69, first runs). After a cold boot met only by ten a second (+0.4 to +4.9, first runs); cap 8 misses at every R (+13.0 to +16.6; inside in at most 1 of 10 runs), and cap 4 at 10,000 is inside in 6 of 10 (+9.5). The first version's "every one of the nine runs at most +9.2" after a restart, and its cold-boot pass at 1,000, came from three runs a cell and did not hold up in ten. **(b) The p99 minus a typical write (the steady state's p50)**: after a restart, ten a second meets it in 5 of 6 first runs, cap 4 at 10,000 in 5 of 10, and cap 8 in 7 of 10 at 1,000, 1 of 10 at 10,000 and 0 of 5 at 100,000; after a cold boot ten a second meets it in 5 of 6 and no cap does. **(c) The longest wait**: nothing on this box meets it reliably, not even the box alone with no transfer (section 8). F-05's other half, "clients retry by themselves", holds here: no write was lost or timed out in 6.45 million (first runs) or 4.38 million (reruns), and a write sent to the old leader was answered `moved` and sent once more.
- **The rules.** V5-7 says the cap adds no rule. Not so: in DEV-10's sense (dropping a rule breaks a stated property), the cap adds +1 in both forms (measured on real code, section 7): the taker's timeout (without it groups were never handed back in 20 of 20 runs with one giver's crash) or, at the giver, "a group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet" (without it 22 of 22 runs left stuck transfers on quiet groups; with it 0 of 6). These are liveness counterexamples on real code at one fault, not model-checked ones, so there is no step count; the fault came 50-200 ms into the hand-back.

## Verdict

Q-53: on real code (E-10's TypeScript Raft on E-26's host, three processes on one box), a capped hand-back evens leaders out in seconds and ten a second in minutes: after one member restarts, 0.1-0.2 s, 0.5-1.3 s and 5-12 s at 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 resources per process with 8 to 128 transfers in flight, against 33 s, 5.6 minutes and (computed) 56 minutes at ten a second; after a whole-project cold boot, 0.1-0.2 s, 0.7-1.7 s and 7-15 s against 24 s, 4.2 minutes and (computed) 41 minutes; in 150 timed hand-backs (and 70 reruns) no transfer failed and no timer election ran; so leaders are even about `settle.ms` plus 1-2 s after a member is back, up to 10,000 (V5-7's "seconds, not minutes" holds, measured). The cost falls on writes while it runs, because the members' thread is busy: other writes are 2-13 ms slower at p99 than with no hand-back at cap 8, and F-05 depends on its reading: as the caught writes' p99 over the steady state's p99, cap 8 after a restart is near the bar (+6 to +8 ms at the median, over it in up to 3 of 10 runs), cap 4 at 10,000 is inside in 10 of 10 (+3.8, for a 2.2 s hand-back instead of 1.4 s), and after a cold boot cap 8 is over at every R (+13 to +17) while cap 4 at 10,000 is inside in 6 of 10; against a typical write or as a longest wait, no cap measured meets it, and the longest-wait reading is out of reach on this box even with no transfer. Caps 32 and 128 cost caught writes 2-9 times more for under a second saved up to 10,000; ten a second costs nothing measurable; no write was lost. Recommended default: cap 8, busiest first, or cap 4 where F-05 must hold under reading (a) (all loaded groups back 0.02-0.10 s into the hand-back at the median). Moves: V5-7 holds with its cap set to 4 to 8, not "a few dozen"; its "no rule is added" does not hold in either form: a cap counted at the taker needs +1 rule (a transfer not won within `election.ms` plus jitter frees its slot and is tried again; without it groups were never handed back in 20 of 20 runs where a giver died mid-way, and at cap 8 a member's whole hand-back stopped in 3 of 10), and a cap counted at the giver needs +1 rule too (a group with a transfer in flight does not go quiet, or the same timeout; without it a dead taker's transfers stayed set on quiet groups in 22 of 22 runs, so their slots never free and their writes wait); the design pushes from the leader, so a cap per taker there would need a `busy` answer and a back-off besides (about +3 to +4, reasoned, not run); no SIMPLICITY record counts the hand-back, so a new record `handback@per-resource` is proposed at 4 rules (3 to 7) with this +1 inside it; `settle.ms` (30 s) is now most of the time leaders stay uneven after a restart; F-05's handover bar holds after a restart only under reading (a), at cap 4 firmly and at cap 8 nearly, and not after a cold boot; no row of the study moves.

## How to rerun

```
sh run.sh pace      # no load: restart and cold boot, R = 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000, four paces, three runs (one timing hold per run and scenario)
sh run.sh load      # 300 loaded groups: the control, cap $BEST random, cap 8 / 32 / 128 busiest first, ten a second busiest first (BEST=32)
sh run.sh settle    # settle.ms 30 s, cap 8, R = 100,000, both scenarios
sh run.sh liveness  # a giver dies mid hand-back, with and without the 3 s timeout (under $SP/bin/heavy; not a timing)
sh run.sh recheck   # after the skeptic's check: F-05's cells at cap 8 (10 runs; 5 at 100,000) and cap 4 at 10,000, met writes marked, like-for-like control (one timing hold per scenario)
sh run.sh recheck-live  # after the skeptic's check: cap 8 when a giver dies, and the givers' side as designed and with patch 18 (under $SP/bin/heavy)
sh run.sh summary   # results/tables.md
sh run.sh all
```

`SP` is the scratchpad (default as in E-26 and E-49). The host is written to `$SP/e53/host/host.ts` each time handback.mjs starts. A run that already has a whole line in `results/` is skipped, so a stopped target resumes; drop a cut last line first. Timed on 2026-11-14: one `load` hold (one run of one scenario, every configuration) took 9-10 minutes, and the whole `settle` target 10 minutes. `pace` and `load` are six holds each, so about an hour each plus any wait for the lock; `liveness` was not timed (20 runs, each up to about a minute). `recheck-live` took 24 minutes (26 runs) and the two `recheck` holds 37 and 36 minutes, each including about 10 minutes lost to one run that could not start (a random port was already in use; it waits 10 minutes for the hosts, fails, and runs again in the next round). The R = 100,000 runs need about 5 GB of RAM. The host now carries patches 15-18 (patch 18 only when named), and client records have five fields, so a rerun of `pace` or `load` runs the fixed host; the first results were taken before patches 15-17 (patches 15 and 16 change nothing in those runs, reasoned in Method).
